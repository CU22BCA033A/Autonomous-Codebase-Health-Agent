# VOLUME 3 — DRAWING & CREATIVE PREPARATION

> **The single most important truth:** NID does **not** reward photorealism. It rewards **clear thinking made visible**: observation, ideas, storytelling, empathy, composition and communication. A confident, well-observed, slightly rough drawing with a strong idea beats a polished drawing of a weak idea. [INFERRED from the nature of NID questions and consistent with NID's stated aims]

## 1. What NID examiners are likely looking for [INFERRED]

| Criterion | What it means | Self-check question |
|---|---|---|
| **Observation** | Real-life details that make a drawing believable | "Would someone from this place recognise it?" |
| **Idea quality** | A thoughtful, relevant interpretation | "Is my idea better than the first thing everyone thinks of?" |
| **Originality** | A personal, unexpected angle | "Would 100 other students draw this?" |
| **Communication** | The message is clear in about 5 seconds | "Can someone explain my drawing back to me?" |
| **Composition** | The frame is used well; there is a focal point | "Where does the eye land first?" |
| **Proportion** | Correct relative sizes | "Is the person taller than the door? (They shouldn't be.)" |
| **Clarity** | Clean lines; labels where useful | "Is anything confusing?" |
| **Storytelling** | Action, emotion, cause and effect | "What is happening, and why?" |
| **Problem-solving** | A solution that fits the user and context | "Does it actually solve the problem for this user?" |

## 2. Tools & materials (exam kit)
- **Pencils:** HB (construction), 2B (outlines), 4B–6B (dark accents).
- **Eraser:** a plastic eraser plus a kneaded eraser (to lift highlights).
- **Black fine-liners:** 0.3 and 0.5 mm, for confident final lines.
- **Colour:** 24-shade colour pencils (layer them; don't press hard at first) and oil pastels (for big areas and posters). Use sketch pens for posters only, sparingly.
- **Other:** a 30 cm scale, a sharpener, and a watch (if permitted).
- **Practice paper:** an A4 sketchbook (80–100 sheets) plus newsprint for warm-ups.

## 3. Core drawing skills

### 3.1 Line quality
- **What:** confident, varied lines. Thick lines for outlines and near objects; thin for details and distance.
- **Rule:** draw from the shoulder or elbow for long lines and from the wrist for details. Use "ghosting": move the hand over the line path 2–3 times, then draw it.
- **Exercises:**
  1. Fill a page with parallel straight lines (horizontal, vertical, diagonal), 5 minutes daily for 2 weeks.
  2. Draw 50 ellipses of different sizes in boxes.
  3. Line-weight drill: redraw a simple cup, varying the weight (heavy on the shadow side and contact points).

### 3.2 Shapes & forms
- Everything can be built from **cubes, cylinders, spheres and cones**. A pressure cooker = cylinder + dome + a handle (an elongated box). A bus = a long box. A person = an oval head + a box torso + cylinder limbs.
- **Exercises:**
  1. Draw 10 household objects, starting from basic forms.
  2. Rotate a cube in 6 positions.
  3. Combine 2 forms into a new object (e.g. a cylinder + a cone becomes a lamp).

### 3.3 Proportion
- **Sight-size and measuring:** use a pencil at arm's length to compare heights and widths.
- **Human proportions:** an adult is about **7.5–8 heads tall**; a child of 5–6 years is about 5–6 heads. The halfway point of an adult is the hips/crotch. With arms down, the fingertips reach mid-thigh.
- **Indian context references:**

| Object | Approximate size |
|---|---|
| Door | 2.1 m |
| Auto-rickshaw | ~1.7 m tall, 2.6 m long |
| Two-wheeler | ~1.1 m tall |
| Cycle-rickshaw seat | ~0.9 m |
| Bus | ~3 m tall |
| Chair seat | ~0.45 m |
| Table | ~0.75 m |
| Kitchen platform | ~0.85 m |

- **Exercise:** draw a person standing beside a door, an auto, a scooter and a bus (4 small studies). Check the ratios against the table.

### 3.4 Light & shadow (more in §7)
- Identify the **light source**. Shade the planes facing away. Add the **cast shadow** on the ground, opposite the light.
- Elements: highlight, light, halftone, core shadow, reflected light, cast shadow.
- **Exercise:** draw a sphere, a cube and a cylinder lit from the top-left; render them with a 5-value scale.

## 4. Perspective scenes (VERY HIGH priority)

**The 6-step scene method (15–20 minutes)**
1. **Decide the story** (30 sec): "Evening vegetable market; a vendor weighs tomatoes for an old woman."
2. **Draw the horizon line** at eye level. Place a VP (1-point) or 2 VPs (2-point).
3. **Block in big shapes:** buildings, road, stalls, as boxes in perspective.
4. **Add people:** heads on the HL for standing adults; place them at different depths (foreground, mid-ground, background). Overlap them.
5. **Add details that tell the story:** weighing scale, tomatoes, cloth bag, price board in Hindi/English, stray dog, bicycle, wires, light bulbs on the stalls.
6. **Finish:** a darker line weight in front, light tone or colour, shadows consistent with the light source.

**Scene checklist:** focal action ✓ · 3 depth layers ✓ · 5+ people with different actions ✓ · context details ✓ · consistent light ✓.

**Exercises (do 1 per day, rotating):**

| # | Scene | Perspective |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A railway platform with a train arriving | 1-point |
| 2 | Your classroom from the last bench | 1-point |
| 3 | A street corner tea stall | 2-point |
| 4 | A three-storey building cut open (a section view showing life on each floor) | 1-point frontal, stacked |
| 5 | A busy bus stop during rain | 2-point |
| 6 | A crowded temple or mosque entrance during a festival | 1-point |
| 7 | A hospital waiting area | 2-point |
| 8 | A rooftop on Makar Sankranti (kite festival) | 3-point (bird's-eye) |
| 9 | A view looking up at a metro bridge | 3-point (worm's-eye) |
| 10 | A narrow lane in an old city (like Varanasi) | 1-point, steep |

**The "three-storey scene" (reported in DAT 2026):** draw a building in **section** (front wall removed, like a doll's house). Show a different activity on each floor: a shop on the ground floor, a family dinner on the 1st, a student studying on the 2nd, and a terrace with clothes drying and water tanks. Connect the floors with a staircase showing a person moving between levels: that creates the story. Keep the floor heights consistent (about 3 m, so about 1.7 people tall per floor).

## 5. Viewpoints & imagination

| Viewpoint | How to draw | Example prompt |
|---|---|---|
| **Bird's-eye** | High horizon (or off the page); 3rd VP below; we see tops of heads and roofs | "Your street as seen by a kite" |
| **Worm's / ant's-eye** | Very low horizon; 3rd VP above; huge foreground objects | "A cricket match seen by an ant on the pitch" |
| **Crow's-view** (DAT 2025 poster) | Elevated, slightly angled; a crow's perspective | "Poster for an orange juice corner from a crow's view" |
| **Through an object** | A frame within the frame | "The world seen through a keyhole / a spectacle lens" |
| **Microscopic** | Exaggerated scale; textures become landscapes | "A day in the life of a dust particle on your desk" |
| **Inside an object** | A cutaway | "Inside a pressure cooker" |
| **Emotional viewpoint** | Distortion that conveys feeling | "The exam hall as seen by a nervous student" |

**Imagination drills:**
- "What if" prompts: what if buildings could walk?
- Combine 2 objects into 1 creature.
- Show a familiar place 100 years in the future.
- Personify objects: the life of a forgotten umbrella.

## 6. Human figures, faces, hands, expressions, gestures

### 6.1 Gesture & stick figures (most important for scenes)
- Start with the **line of action**, a single curve showing the pose's energy.
- Then draw a **bean** torso (chest + pelvis with a twist), oval head, cylinder limbs, and simple hands and feet.
- **Exercise:** 30-second gestures × 20 daily (people in videos, family members, or photos of crowds).

### 6.2 Body proportions & poses
- Draw standing, sitting (cross-legged on the floor, a common Indian pose), squatting, walking, running, bending, lifting, carrying a load on the head, cycling, and riding pillion.
- **Balance rule:** for a standing figure, the head is roughly above the weight-bearing foot.

### 6.3 Faces & expressions
- Face proportions: the eyes sit halfway down the head; the nose halfway between the eyes and the chin; the mouth a third of the way from nose to chin.
- **Expressions via eyebrows + mouth:**

| Emotion | Eyebrows | Mouth |
|---|---|---|
| Happy | relaxed | upturned |
| Sad | inner ends raised | down |
| Angry | inner ends down (V-shape) | tight or showing teeth |
| Surprised | high | open "O" |
| Afraid | raised and tense | stretched |
| Disgust | lowered | upper lip raised, nose wrinkled |

- **Exercise:** a 6-emotion grid with the same character, daily for a week.

### 6.4 Hands
- Treat the hand as a **mitten** shape first, then split the fingers. The palm is about as long as the middle finger.
- **Exercise:** draw your non-drawing hand in 10 poses (gripping a cup, pointing, holding a phone, fist, writing).

### 6.5 Indian people & clothing details
Saree pleats, kurta, dhoti, salwar, school uniforms, turbans (regional styles), a gamchha on the shoulder, chappals, a police uniform, a vendor's apron, a construction worker's helmet, a doctor's coat.

## 7. Light, shadow, texture & material representation

| Material | How to show it |
|---|---|
| **Glass** | Strong, sharp highlights; see-through background with slight distortion; reflections at the edges |
| **Polished metal** (steel tiffin) | High contrast; reflected surroundings as bands; bright highlights next to dark |
| **Matte plastic** | Soft gradients; small soft highlights |
| **Wood** | Grain lines following the form; warm tones |
| **Fabric** | Folds radiating from points of tension (shoulders, knees); soft shadows |
| **Water** | Horizontal reflections; ripples; transparency |
| **Terracotta / clay** | Matte, warm; rough stippled texture |
| **Concrete / stone** | Speckle texture; cracks |
| **Rubber** | Dark, matte; soft highlights |
| **Leaves / foliage** | Clusters rather than single leaves; light on top, dark beneath |

**Exercise:** draw the same cylinder in 6 materials: glass, steel, wood, clay, fabric-covered and rubber.

## 8. Product sketching & redesign (VERY HIGH priority)

### 8.1 The 5-step redesign method ("PUCIS")
1. **P**roblem: list 4–6 problems with the current product (observe users).
2. **U**ser: who is the user? Age, abilities, context (e.g. an elderly user on a train).
3. **C**onstraints: cost, material, manufacturing, space, electricity, water, culture.
4. **I**deas: 3 thumbnail concepts, each 1–2 minutes.
5. **S**ketch the selected concept: a big main view (3/4 perspective) + 1–2 detail views + labels + a small "in use" scene + a 1–2 line justification.

### 8.2 Product sketch layout for an answer box

```
+--------------------------------------------------------+
|  TITLE: "EasyPour Pressure Cooker for Elderly Users"   |
|  [Problems: 1. heavy  2. hot handle  3. whistle fear]  |
|                                                        |
|        [ BIG 3/4 VIEW OF PRODUCT ]      [detail 1]     |
|          labels with arrows →           [detail 2]     |
|                                                        |
|  [small in-use scene: user lifting with two handles]   |
|  Materials: ...    How it helps: ...                   |
+--------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 8.3 Product sketching technique
- Use 3/4 view (two-point) for most products; add a front or side elevation if helpful.
- Ghost the construction lines lightly, then darken the final silhouette.
- Show **mechanisms** with arrows (rotate, slide, fold) and **exploded views** for assembly.
- Show **scale** by adding a hand holding the product.

### 8.4 Products to practise redesigning (common Indian context)
pressure cooker · bucket and mug · water bottle for school kids · school bag · auto-rickshaw seat · bus stop · street-vendor cart · tiffin · steel almirah · ceiling fan cleaning · chappal · umbrella · gas cylinder trolley · clothes-drying stand for balconies · walking stick · pill organiser · broom · dustbin with segregation · railway berth ladder · hospital stretcher · temple shoe stand · public water tap · mobile charging kiosk · tea kettle for a chaiwala · kite spool (charkha) · rangoli tool · bicycle carrier.

## 9. Visual communication

### 9.1 Poster process (20–35 min)
1. **Message** in one sentence ("Drink fresh orange juice at Sunny Corner").
2. **Audience & tone** (kids? commuters? fun or serious?).
3. **Big idea:** a visual metaphor or twist (the orange as the sun, seen by a crow from above).
4. **Thumbnails:** 3 layouts at 3×4 cm.
5. **Final:** headline (big, 3–6 words), one dominant image, a supporting line, a call to action and logo, a limited palette (3–4 colours), and clear hierarchy.

**Visual metaphor bank:**
- Earth as a melting ice cream (climate)
- A tree with lungs as foliage (air)
- A tap dripping hourglass sand (water scarcity)
- A pencil as a rocket (education)
- A seat belt as a hug (road safety)

### 9.2 Logo / symbol process
1. Brainstorm keywords (the name, values, activity).
2. Look for **double meanings and negative space**.
3. Draw 10 quick thumbnails.
4. Select and refine using geometric construction.
5. Test it small (1 cm) and in black and white.
6. Show the final in colour plus one application (signboard, cup, app icon).

### 9.3 Signage set (e.g. "5 signboards for narrow streets," DAT 2026)
- Use a **consistent system**: the same shape family, colour code, icon style and font.
- Show the **sign in context**: one small drawing of a sign mounted in the lane, at a believable height.
- Prioritise icons with minimal bilingual text.

### 9.4 Packaging
- Show a dieline (net) + the 3D form + the front panel design + the opening mechanism.
- Consider protection, stacking, the user's grip, reuse after use, and material (paper, cloth, leaf).

## 10. Sequential drawing & visual storytelling (VERY HIGH priority)

**Rules**
1. **Plan the beats first:** setup → problem → action → resolution (4 frames), or add build-up frames for 6.
2. **Continuity:** the same character design (clothes, hair, props) and setting in every frame.
3. **Vary the shots:** wide (establishing), medium (action), close-up (emotion or detail).
4. **Use the 180° rule:** keep the camera on one side of the action, so characters don't swap sides.
5. **Show emotion** through faces and body language.
6. **Minimal text:** captions of 3–6 words if allowed; speech bubbles are sparing.
7. **Number the frames** and keep the reading order left→right, top→bottom.

**Worked plan: "Helping an elderly person who falls during a morning walk" (DAT 2026, reported)**

| Frame | Shot | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wide | A park at dawn, joggers, an elderly man walking with a stick; a loose paving stone visible (foreshadowing) |
| 2 | Medium | He trips on the stone; the stick flies; a young girl notices |
| 3 | Close-up | His pained face; the girl kneels and asks "Are you okay?"; her phone in hand |
| 4 | Medium | She helps him sit on a bench; another walker brings water; she calls his family / 108 ambulance |
| 5 | Medium | Family arrives; gratitude; the girl smiles |
| 6 | Wide / epilogue | Next morning, the paving stone is fixed and a "Mind your step" sign is placed: the systemic solution. This shows design thinking. |

**Exercises:** 2 stories per week from the prompt bank (§13, category C).

## 11. Memory drawing
- **What:** drawing objects, places or events from memory, accurately and with characteristic details.
- **Method:** recall the overall form → key proportions → the defining details (logo position, buttons, joints) → the context of use.
- **Weekly drill:** draw 5 objects from memory, then compare them with reality and circle the errors in red. Common subjects:
  - pressure cooker
  - ceiling fan
  - auto-rickshaw
  - bicycle (chain on the right; pedals; seat; stand)
  - school bench
  - railway coach interior
  - steel glass
  - matchbox
  - water cooler
  - post box (red, cylindrical)
  - traffic signal
  - gas stove
  - street lamp
  - your own house's entrance
- **Food illustration (DAT 2026: shikanji / thandai):** show the vessel (steel glass or kulhad), the ingredients around it (lemon slices, mint, ice, black salt; for thandai: almonds, saffron, rose petals, fennel), condensation droplets, the serving context (a street cart, a Holi setting), and labels. Colour helps a lot here.

## 12. Mains preparation

### 12.1 Studio Sensitivity Test
**Skills:**
- clay modelling (pinch, coil, slab)
- paper engineering (folding, pop-up, scoring, curling)
- wire forms
- found-object assemblage
- material textures

**Weekly practice (start right after Prelims, or earlier on weekends):**
1. Clay: model 3 animals in different expressions (30 min).
2. Paper: create a structure that holds a 500 g weight using only 2 A4 sheets.
3. Wire: make a figure showing movement (dancing, running).
4. Theme models: "Peace", "Speed", "Friendship", "Growth", "Balance". Interpret abstractly and literally.
5. Explain each model in 3 lines (concept, material choice, what it shows).

**Evaluation logic [INFERRED]:** concept + craft + material sensitivity + use of time.

### 12.2 In-Person Sensitivity Test (interview-style)
**Prepare answers (spoken, 1–2 min each) for:**
1. Why design? Why NID?
2. Which discipline and why?
3. A product you love or hate, and why. Have a redesign idea ready.
4. A craft from your state: its process, the community, its challenges.
5. A recent design or architecture news item (Vol 11).
6. Your hobbies and what you observe through them.
7. A problem in your city you would like to solve.
8. Your Studio Test model: explain it.

**Tips:** be honest; think aloud; give specific examples; it's fine to say "I don't know, but I think…".

## 13. THE 110-PROMPT SUBJECTIVE BANK

Each prompt lists: **Consider** (what to think about) · **Composition** · **Idea strategy** · **Common mistakes** · **Evaluation criteria**. Suggested time: 15–25 min each unless stated. The "Evaluation" line names what an examiner would look for. Use it to self-score out of 10.

### A. Draw from Memory / Observation (A1–A12)

**A1. Draw a pressure cooker from memory, showing how the whistle works.**
- Consider: body, lid with gasket, handle(s), whistle weight, safety valve, base.
- Composition: a large 3/4 view + a cutaway of the whistle/vent.
- Idea: a cutaway with steam arrows.
- Mistakes: forgetting the gasket and safety valve; the handle on the wrong side.
- Evaluation: accuracy, mechanism clarity, labels, proportion.

**A2. Draw the inside of a railway sleeper coach from the aisle.**
- Consider: berths (upper, middle, lower, side), ladder, windows with bars, fans, chains, luggage, passengers.
- Composition: 1-point perspective down the aisle.
- Idea: show a story, e.g. a family sharing food.
- Mistakes: incorrect berth arrangement; a flat drawing without perspective.
- Evaluation: perspective, observation, life.

**A3. Draw an auto-rickshaw from a 3/4 front view.**
- Consider: the three wheels, single headlight (older models) or twin, canopy, meter, driver seat, passenger bench.
- Composition: 2-point perspective.
- Idea: add a driver and a passenger to show scale.
- Mistakes: four wheels; wrong canopy shape.
- Evaluation: form, proportion, detail.

**A4. Draw your school's main gate at 8 am.**
- Consider: guard, students, parents, vehicles, vendors.
- Composition: a frontal or angled perspective.
- Idea: show the morning rush.
- Mistakes: an empty scene.
- Evaluation: observation, activity, depth.

**A5. Draw a vegetable vendor's cart with at least 8 identifiable vegetables.**
- Consider: the weighing scale, umbrella, cart wheels, vendor, customer.
- Composition: 2-point perspective.
- Idea: show bargaining.
- Mistakes: vegetables that can't be identified.
- Evaluation: identifiability, colour, story.

**A6. Draw a bicycle accurately, side view.**
- Consider: the diamond frame, chain on the right, pedals, brakes, carrier, bell, stand.
- Composition: side elevation with accurate proportion.
- Idea: add a milk can on the carrier for context.
- Mistakes: the chain on the wrong side; a missing front fork; wrong frame triangles.
- Evaluation: accuracy.

**A7. Draw a ceiling fan as seen from directly below it.**
- Consider: the hub, 3 blades with a radial arrangement, the canopy, the down-rod foreshortened.
- Idea: show motion blur in one version.
- Mistakes: drawing a side view instead.
- Evaluation: viewpoint accuracy.

**A8. Draw the dining table of your house after dinner.**
- Consider: utensils, leftovers, cloth, objects that tell a story about the family.
- Idea: details that reveal the people.
- Mistakes: a sterile, empty table.
- Evaluation: storytelling through objects.

**A9. Draw a "product you love" and write 200 words about it (DAT 2026, reported).**
- Consider: the object's form, material, function, emotion, sustainability, and one improvement.
- Composition: a big 3/4 view + a small in-use frame; the text in a neat block.
- Idea: choose a humble, meaningful product (tiffin, grandfather's radio, a pencil box) over a flashy phone.
- Mistakes: an iPhone with generic text; going over or under the word count.
- Evaluation: drawing quality, depth of observation, writing structure (see Vol 2B §3.5).

**A10. Illustrate a glass of shikanji or thandai with its ingredients (DAT 2026, reported).**
- Consider: the vessel, condensation, ingredients arranged around it, the context (street cart or festival), labels.
- Composition: the vessel at a thirds point; ingredients in an arc.
- Idea: an "ingredients journey" with arrows into the glass.
- Mistakes: no colour; ingredients unlabelled.
- Evaluation: appetite appeal, accuracy, layout.

**A11. Draw a market scene during a power cut at night.**
- Consider: light sources (candles, phone torches, petromax lamps, an inverter-lit shop), long shadows.
- Composition: strong value contrast.
- Idea: one shop with an inverter glowing, the others dark.
- Mistakes: lighting the whole scene evenly.
- Evaluation: handling of light and shadow, mood.

**A12. Draw your study table as it is right now, without tidying.**
- Consider: honesty, clutter, the materials.
- Idea: a revealing self-portrait through objects.
- Evaluation: observation, composition.

### B. Scenes & Perspective (B1–B14)

**B1. A three-storey building with life on every floor (DAT 2026 theme, reported).**
- Consider: a section view; different activities on each floor (ground: a shop; 1st: a family; 2nd: a student; terrace: water tanks and clothes lines); a staircase linking them.
- Composition: frontal 1-point; stack the floors.
- Idea: one character moving between floors (e.g. a cat chasing a ball down the stairs) ties it together.
- Mistakes: inconsistent floor heights; tiny people.
- Evaluation: perspective, proportion, storytelling.

**B2. A railway platform as a train arrives, from the footbridge.**
- Consider: bird's-eye (3-point downward), the train roof, the crowd surging, porters, the tea vendor.
- Mistakes: wrong VP placement.
- Evaluation: viewpoint, crowd handling.

**B3. A flooded street during the monsoon.**
- Consider: water levels on people (ankle/knee), reflections, umbrellas, rickshaws, plastic sheets over shops, kids playing with paper boats.
- Evaluation: atmosphere, observation.

**B4. A government hospital OPD waiting area.**
- Consider: queues, a token display, a fan, posters, a wheelchair, anxious faces.
- Idea: highlight one design problem (e.g. no seating for the elderly).
- Evaluation: empathy, observation.

**B5. A wedding procession (baraat) in a narrow lane.**
- Consider: the band, the groom on a horse, lights carried by workers, onlookers on balconies.
- Composition: 1-point down the lane.
- Evaluation: energy, depth.

**B6. A view from inside a moving bus window.**
- Consider: a frame within a frame, motion blur outside, the passengers' reflections.
- Evaluation: viewpoint creativity.

**B7. A rooftop on Uttarayan / Makar Sankranti (kite festival).**
- Consider: bird's-eye or rooftop level; many kites; people with spools; the neighbouring rooftops.
- Evaluation: perspective, crowd, colour.

**B8. A school sports day seen from the top of the stands.**
- Consider: track lanes in perspective, crowds, flags.
- Evaluation: perspective of curved lanes.

**B9. A street-food lane at 9 pm.**
- Consider: light from stalls, steam, signage, crowds.
- Evaluation: lighting, density.

**B10. The view an ant sees on a picnic mat.**
- Consider: worm's-eye; giant food items; legs like towers.
- Evaluation: scale manipulation, imagination.

**B11. The interior of a traditional Indian kitchen vs a modular kitchen (split frame).**
- Consider: a chulha / tawa vs a chimney and hob; floor-seated vs standing work.
- Evaluation: observation, comparison.

**B12. A post office queue (or bank queue) and its problems.**
- Consider: annotate the pain points; propose a fix in an inset.
- Evaluation: problem identification.

**B13. A village well or handpump in the evening.**
- Consider: women with pots, children, animals, the setting sun, long shadows.
- Evaluation: composition, light.

**B14. A metro station entrance seen from below, at street level.**
- Consider: worm's-eye; 3-point upward perspective.
- Evaluation: technical perspective.

### D. Product Design / Redesign (D1–D16)

**D1. Items to carry while travelling with your grandmother; sketch and organise them (DAT 2026, reported).**
- Consider: medicines, spectacles, a shawl, water, a cushion, documents, a phone with large numbers, snacks, a walking stick, a pill timer; plus a carry solution.
- Composition: a flat-lay "knolling" (items arranged in a neat grid) + one organiser product.
- Idea: a colour-coded travel pouch with large-print labels.
- Mistakes: listing items without design thought.
- Evaluation: empathy, completeness, organisation.

### Self-scoring rubric for any prompt (out of 10)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Followed instructions (count, viewpoint, constraints) | missed | partly | fully |
| Idea (relevance + originality) | cliché / off-topic | sensible | fresh and fitting |
| Observation & detail | generic | some | rich, believable |
| Composition & proportion | poor | acceptable | strong |
| Communication (clarity, labels, writing) | confusing | readable | instantly clear |

Score bands: 9–10 = excellent; 7–8 = strong; 5–6 = developing; below 5 = redo the prompt within 3 days. These are practice benchmarks, not NID's actual scoring.
