## PART II — SUBJECTIVE (70 marks)

**Q23. Expressive Typography + Logo (15 marks)**
(a) Write each of the words **MELT, FLY, CRACK** and **ECHO** so that its appearance expresses its meaning (8 marks).
(b) Design a logo for a monsoon festival called **"BAARISH"**. Show it in colour and in single-colour (black) versions (7 marks).

**Q24. Bird's-Eye Storytelling (20 marks)**
A pigeon sits on the roof girder of a busy railway station and looks down. Draw what it sees in **bird's-eye perspective**. Within the crowd, include **one small story** the pigeon notices (for example, someone helping, a reunion, a chase). Make the story findable but not obvious.

**Q25. Packaging with Dieline (15 marks)**
Design **eco-friendly packaging for six kulhads** (clay cups) to be sent by courier. Show: (a) the flat net (dieline) with fold and cut lines, (b) the assembled package, (c) how each kulhad is protected from breaking, and (d) a simple graphic identity on the box.

**Q26. Service / System Design (20 marks)**
At a very large festival gathering (like the Kumbh Mela), many people, especially children and the elderly, get separated from their families.
(a) Identify **three user groups** and one problem for each (4 marks).
(b) Design a **lost-and-found system**: include an identification idea (e.g. a wristband or token), a booth design and signage (10 marks).
(c) Show in a **3-frame storyboard** how a lost child is reunited using your system (6 marks).

## DETAILED SOLUTIONS — PART I

**Q1 → (b) Matisse.** Monet = Impressionism; Dalí = Surrealism; Munch = Expressionism. *Diff:* E.

**Q2 → (b) double ikat.** Both the warp and weft threads are resist-dyed before weaving, so the pattern appears on both sides. *Diff:* M.

**Q3 → (b) 130°.** θ = |30H − 5.5M| = |90 − 220| = 130°. *Diff:* M.

**Q4 → (d) Flying buttress.** That is a Gothic element. The five points are pilotis, free plan, free façade, horizontal windows and roof garden. *Diff:* M.

**Q5 → (a) NOON.** A 180° rotation reverses the letter order and turns each letter upside down. N and O both look the same upside down, and NOON is the same in reverse, so it reads NOON again. MOON becomes NOOW, because M turns into W. SOON becomes NOOS, and the B in BOON doesn't survive. *Diff:* H.

**Q6 → (c) 12.** Euler's formula V − E + F = 2 gives 6 − E + 8 = 2, so E = 12. *Diff:* M.

**Q7 → (b) freehand pen.** Machilipatnam Kalamkari is the block-printed style. *Diff:* M.

**Q8 → (c) Bodoni.** It is a high-contrast serif (Didone) typeface; the other three are sans serif. *Diff:* M.

**Q9 → (b) split-complementary.** The complement of blue is orange; its neighbours red-orange and yellow-orange are used instead. *Diff:* M.

**Q10 → (b) Narasimhadeva I** of the Eastern Ganga dynasty, 13th century. Rajaraja Chola I built Thanjavur's Brihadeeswara. *Diff:* M.

**Q11 → (b) 21.** These are triangular numbers, with differences +2, +3, +4, +5, +6. *Diff:* E.

**Q12 → (b) Closure.** The mind fills the gaps between the stripes. *Diff:* M.

**Q13 → (b) Netherlands.** *Diff:* E.

**Q14 → (b).** Aluminium is light, does not rust and can be recycled repeatedly with large energy savings. *Diff:* E.

**Q15 → (c) 18.** Each triangle uses 2 of the 4 apex lines plus one horizontal "base". There are C(4,2) = 6 pairs of lines and 3 bases (the 2 crossing lines + the actual base), so 6 × 3 = 18. *Diff:* H.

**Q16 → (c) (3, 1).** The fold line is x + y = 8. Reflecting (x, y) across it gives (8 − y, 8 − x) = (8 − 5, 8 − 7) = (3, 1). *Check:* the midpoint of (7,5) and (3,1) is (5,3), which lies on x + y = 8 ✓. The trap is (5, 7), which comes from swapping coordinates as if the fold were y = x. *Diff:* H.

**Q17 → (b) 4.** One-face cubes = 2[(a−2)(b−2) + (b−2)(c−2) + (a−2)(c−2)] with a = 2, b = 3, c = 4. That is 2[0 × 1 + 1 × 2 + 0 × 2] = 2 × 2 = 4. *Check:* corners 8 + two-face 4[(0) + (1) + (2)] = 12 + one-face 4 + inside 0 = 24 ✓. *Diff:* H.

**Q18 → (b) A.** The order is E > C > A > B > D. *Diff:* M.

**Q19 → (b) 45 cm × 10 cm.** Circumference = 2 × 22/7 × 7 = 44 cm; adding 1 cm of overlap gives 45 cm. Height 10 cm. *Diff:* M.

**Q20 → (b).** A continuous guiding cue (floor lines) plus signs at the decision points matches how people navigate. (c) overloads memory; (a) and (d) don't solve wayfinding. *Diff:* M.

**Q21 → (b).** *Diff:* E.

**Q22 → (c) 12.** The large gear's 4 turns pass 4 × 45 = 180 teeth; 180 ÷ 15 = 12 turns of the small gear. *Diff:* M.

## DETAILED SOLUTIONS — PART II

### Q23. Expressive typography + BAARISH logo (15 marks)

**(a) Model treatments (2 marks each)**
- **MELT:** bold letters whose lower halves drip and pool into a puddle; the "T" bends.
- **FLY:** light letters rising diagonally upward; the "Y" becomes a bird's wing shape, with small motion lines.
- **CRACK:** solid block letters split by a jagged crack running through all five; small fragments falling.
- **ECHO:** the word repeated 3–4 times, each repetition lighter and smaller, receding (a fading repeat).

**(b) Logo "BAARISH" (7 marks)**
- *Concept:* the two **A**s become two **raindrops** (inverted teardrops with the crossbar as a ripple). Alternatively, the dot of the **i** becomes a falling drop that splashes into ripple lines under the word.
- *Colour version:* monsoon teal and deep indigo, with a fresh green accent.
- *Single-colour version:* all black on white, to prove it works without colour.
- *Typeface:* a rounded sans for friendliness.

**Rubric:** (a) 8 = 4 words × 2 (meaning conveyed + legibility). (b) 7 = concept 3 + execution 2 + mono version working 2.

**Mistakes:** illegible distortions; simply colouring the words instead of transforming their form; a logo that collapses in black and white.

### Q24. Pigeon's-eye railway station (20 marks)

**Approach**
- **Perspective:** 3-point, looking down. The third VP is below the page, the platforms converge, and we see the tops of heads, umbrellas, luggage, the train roof and the overhead wires in the foreground (the pigeon's girder can frame the top edge).
- **Crowd:** 25+ simplified figures shown as head-and-shoulder ovals from above, with long luggage shadows (for example, late-afternoon light).
- **Hidden story options:**
  - a child's balloon escaping, with someone reaching for it
  - a porter helping an elderly woman with a heavy trunk across the footbridge
  - two friends running towards each other for a reunion
  - a pickpocket being chased by a policeman, shown as a line of displaced people
- **Findability:** place the story at a thirds intersection; give the main characters a single accent colour (e.g. a red dupatta) while the rest of the crowd is muted.

**Rubric (20)**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Correct bird's-eye perspective | 6 |
| Crowd handling & variety | 4 |
| Hidden story: clarity & cleverness | 4 |
| Composition & use of accent | 3 |
| Station details (tracks, platform, signs, train roof) | 3 |

**Mistakes:** drawing an eye-level view; making the story so central that it isn't "hidden"; repeating identical figures.

### Q25. Kulhad packaging (15 marks)

**Model concept: "Matti-Pack"**
- **Structure:** a corrugated-cardboard box (recycled kraft) with an internal **die-cut honeycomb insert**: 6 conical cut-outs arranged 3 × 2 that cradle each kulhad upside down. The kulhads' rims rest on a **molded-pulp (egg-tray type) bed** or on crumpled newspaper/rice straw.
- **Dieline:** the box net shows solid lines = cut, dashed lines = fold, glue flaps marked; a separate insert net with 6 circular cut-outs and slit tabs that lock into the walls.
- **Assembled view:** 3/4 view with the lid open, showing the kulhads nested.
- **Protection:** each kulhad is isolated (no clay-on-clay contact); there is a 1.5 cm air gap from the walls; a "FRAGILE – this side up" pictogram; no plastic.
- **Graphic identity:** a terracotta-orange band with a simple line icon of a kulhad and steam, a handmade-craft story line ("Made by the potters of Khurja"), and a recycle symbol.
- **Reuse:** the insert doubles as a 6-cup serving tray.

**Rubric (15)**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Correct, readable dieline | 4 |
| Protection logic | 4 |
| Sustainability (no plastic, reuse) | 3 |
| Graphic identity | 2 |
| Clarity of assembled drawing | 2 |

**Mistakes:** bubble wrap (contradicts eco-friendly); a dieline that cannot fold into the shown box; ignoring breakage risk.

### Q26. Festival lost-and-found system (20 marks)

**(a) User groups & problems (4)**
1. **Young children:** cannot state their address or phone number.
2. **Elderly people:** get confused, may have poor hearing or memory, may not carry a phone.
3. **Rural first-time visitors:** language barriers; unfamiliar with announcements and digital systems.
4. *(optional)* **Volunteers/police:** need quick identification without a common language.

**(b) System design (10)**
- **ID: "Milan Band"** (milan = reunion). At the entry gates, families receive matching coloured wristbands. Each band carries a family number printed in large digits, plus a QR code linked to the guardian's phone number. A colour indicates the sector of the camp where the family is staying. The bands are tear-resistant Tyvek and waterproof.
- **Booths:** tall, visible "Milan Kendra" towers every 500 m, with a beacon light and a large handshake icon. Each has a seating area, water and a public-address system; volunteers scan the band's QR code, which sends an SMS to the guardian; a loudspeaker announcement is made in 3 languages.
- **Signage:** a consistent icon (two hands joining) in bright yellow-and-black, directional arrows at intersections ("Milan Kendra 200 m →"), and floor stencils.
- **Low-tech backup:** if the phone network fails, families have pre-agreed meeting points (numbered pillars).

**(c) 3-frame storyboard (6)**
1. A child, crying, is separated in the crowd; a volunteer notices the yellow band.
2. At the Milan Kendra, the volunteer scans the band; the mother's phone buzzes with "Your child is at Milan Kendra 12".
3. A reunion hug at the booth; the band numbers match; a calm environment.

**Rubric (20):** problems 4; ID design 3; booth 3; signage 2; low-tech/backup thinking 2; storyboard clarity 4; overall feasibility 2.

**Mistakes:** an app-only solution (ignores the elderly, rural users and network failure); unclear storyboard continuity; no signage.
