## 2. Indian Folk & Tribal Painting Traditions

| Art form | State / region | Key features | Notable names / facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Madhubani / Mithila** | Bihar (Mithila region) | Bold outlines, no empty space, double lines, fish, peacocks, gods; natural colours; styles include Bharni, Kachni, Tantrik, Godna, Kohbar | Sita Devi, Ganga Devi, Mahasundari Devi, Baua Devi (Padma Shri). GI tag. |
| **Warli** | Maharashtra (Thane, Palghar) | White rice-paste on a mud or red-ochre wall; **circle, triangle, square** (sun/moon, mountains, sacred enclosure); stick figures dancing in a spiral (Tarpa dance) | Jivya Soma Mashe (Padma Shri). Painted by the Warli tribe. |
| **Gond** | Madhya Pradesh | Dots, dashes and lines fill the forms; animals, trees, myths; vivid colours | **Jangarh Singh Shyam** (pioneer), **Bhajju Shyam** (*The London Jungle Book*), Durga Bai |
| **Pithora** | Gujarat / MP (Rathwa, Bhil tribes) | Ritual wall paintings, horses, painted for thanksgiving | — |
| **Bhil** | MP / Rajasthan / Gujarat | Dot patterns (like grains of maize) | Bhuri Bai (Padma Shri) |
| **Saura** | Odisha | Ritual wall paintings (*ittals*), similar to Warli but with more detailed figures | — |
| **Sohrai & Khovar** | Jharkhand (Hazaribagh) | Mural art for harvest (Sohrai) and marriage (Khovar), made with earth colours | GI tag |
| **Pattachitra** | Odisha (Raghurajpur) and West Bengal | Cloth-based scroll paintings; Jagannath themes; fine lines; natural colours; ornate borders | Raghurajpur heritage crafts village |
| **Kalighat** | Kolkata, West Bengal | 19th-century bazaar paintings; bold sweeping brush lines; satire of the babu culture | Painted near the Kalighat Temple |
| **Kalamkari** | Andhra Pradesh | "Kalam" (pen) + "kari" (work). **Srikalahasti style** (freehand pen, mythological) and **Machilipatnam style** (block-printed, Persian motifs) | GI tags for both |
| **Phad** | Rajasthan (Bhilwara) | Long horizontal scrolls narrating the epics of folk deities **Pabuji** and **Devnarayan**; performed by Bhopa priests | Joshi family of painters |
| **Tanjore / Thanjavur** | Tamil Nadu | Gold foil, gems, relief work on wooden boards; Hindu deities; Maratha-period patronage | GI tag |
| **Mysore painting** | Karnataka | Like Tanjore but subtler, with less relief | — |
| **Cheriyal scrolls** | Telangana | Narrative scrolls in a comic-strip style; red backgrounds | GI tag |
| **Thangka** | Sikkim, Ladakh, Himachal | Buddhist painting on cotton or silk; mandalas | — |
| **Manjusha** | Bihar (Bhagalpur) | Snake motifs; Bihula–Bishahari legend; border-based | GI tag |
| **Kalamezhuthu** | Kerala | Ritual floor drawings made with coloured powders | — |
| **Rangoli / Kolam / Alpana / Mandana** | All India (Kolam = TN; Alpana = Bengal; Mandana = Rajasthan) | Floor patterns, often radial, for welcome and festivals | Kolam uses rice flour, a white line-and-dot grid |

**Classic trap:** ikat dyes the **yarn before weaving**; block printing and batik work on **finished cloth**.

**GI (Geographical Indication):** a tag certifying that a product's quality or reputation comes from its place of origin. The GI Registry is in **Chennai**.

## 5. Indian Architecture

### 5.1 Timeline of Indian architecture (with icons)

| Period | Key works | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Indus Valley** (c. 2600–1900 BCE) | Mohenjo-daro (**Great Bath**), Harappa, **Dholavira** (UNESCO 2021), Lothal (dockyard) | Grid town planning, burnt bricks, covered drains |
| **Mauryan** (3rd c. BCE) | **Ashokan pillars**: the Lion Capital of **Sarnath**, India's national emblem; **Sanchi Stupa** (core by Ashoka) | Polished sandstone (the "Mauryan polish") |
| **Buddhist rock-cut** | **Ajanta** (murals, 2nd c. BCE–6th c. CE), **Karla chaitya**, **Ellora** (Buddhist, Hindu and Jain caves) | Chaitya (prayer hall) and vihara (monastery) |
| **Gupta** (4th–6th c.) | Dashavatara Temple (Deogarh), Udayagiri caves | The beginnings of the Hindu temple form |
| **Early medieval temples** | **Kailasa Temple, Ellora (Cave 16)**: carved top-down from a single rock under the Rashtrakuta king **Krishna I** (8th c.). **Pallava Mahabalipuram** (Shore Temple, Rathas, Arjuna's Penance). | Monolithic carving |
| **Nagara style (North)** | **Khajuraho** (Chandela), **Konark Sun Temple** (Odisha, 13th c., King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty; designed as a chariot with 24 wheels and 7 horses), **Lingaraja** (Bhubaneswar), **Modhera Sun Temple** (Solanki) | Curvilinear **shikhara**; no boundary walls; the amalaka on top |
| **Dravida style (South)** | **Brihadeeswara Temple, Thanjavur** (Rajaraja Chola I, completed c. 1010 CE); Meenakshi Temple (Madurai); Hampi (Vijayanagara) | Pyramidal **vimana**; huge **gopurams** (gateways); enclosed courtyards |
| **Vesara (Deccan hybrid)** | **Hoysala temples**: Belur, Halebidu, Somanathapura (the **Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas**, UNESCO 2023); star-shaped plans; soapstone | Mixes Nagara and Dravida |
| **Kakatiya** | **Ramappa (Rudreswara) Temple**, Telangana (UNESCO 2021); floating bricks | — |
| **Stepwells** | **Rani ki Vav**, Patan (11th c., commissioned by Queen Udayamati; UNESCO 2014; shown on the ₹100 note); Adalaj (Gujarat); **Chand Baori** (Abhaneri, Rajasthan) | Water storage and a cooler microclimate |
| **Indo-Islamic (Delhi Sultanate)** | **Qutub Minar** (begun by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, completed by Iltutmish; about 73 m); **Alai Darwaza** (true arch, dome) | True arches, domes, calligraphy, geometric ornament |
| **Provincial Islamic** | **Gol Gumbaz** (Bijapur, Adil Shahi; a huge dome with a whispering gallery); **Charminar** (Hyderabad, 1591, Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah) | — |
| **Mughal** | **Humayun's Tomb** (Delhi, 1560s–70s, commissioned by Bega Begum, architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyas; the **first garden tomb** on the Indian subcontinent; charbagh). **Fatehpur Sikri** (Akbar; Buland Darwaza). **Taj Mahal** (Shah Jahan for Mumtaz Mahal, c. 1632–1653; chief architect usually credited as **Ustad Ahmad Lahauri**; white Makrana marble; pietra dura). **Red Fort** (Delhi). **Jama Masjid** (Delhi). | Charbagh (four-part garden), double domes, symmetry, red sandstone and white marble |
| **Rajput** | **Amber Fort**; **Hawa Mahal** (Jaipur, 1799, Lal Chand Ustad; 953 jharokhas); **Jantar Mantar** (Sawai Jai Singh II; UNESCO); Chittorgarh; Mehrangarh | Jharokha, chhatri, jaali |
| **Colonial / Indo-Saracenic** | **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus** (Mumbai, F.W. Stevens, 1888, Victorian Gothic + Indian; UNESCO); **Gateway of India** (George Wittet, 1924); **Victoria Memorial** (Kolkata, William Emerson); **Rashtrapati Bhavan** (Edwin Lutyens); **North & South Blocks** (Herbert Baker); old **Parliament House** (Lutyens & Baker, circular) | Domes, arches, chhatris + European plans |
| **Modern** | **Chandigarh** (Le Corbusier with Pierre Jeanneret; Capitol Complex part of a UNESCO transnational serial site, 2016); **IIM Ahmedabad** (Louis Kahn; brick arches); **Mill Owners' Association building** and **Sanskar Kendra**, Ahmedabad (Le Corbusier) | Béton brut (raw concrete), brutalism |
| **Indian modernists** | **B.V. Doshi**: Sangath (his studio), IIM Bangalore, **Aranya low-cost housing** (Indore), Amdavad ni Gufa (with M.F. Husain); **Pritzker 2018** (first Indian). **Charles Correa**: Gandhi Smarak Sangrahalaya (Sabarmati Ashram), Jawahar Kala Kendra (Jaipur; navagraha plan), Kanchanjunga Apartments (Mumbai), Navi Mumbai planning. **Laurie Baker**: low-cost brick architecture in Kerala, rat-trap bond, jaali walls. **Raj Rewal**: Hall of Nations (Pragati Maidan, demolished 2017), Asian Games Village. **Achyut Kanvinde**. **Anant Raje**. **Joseph Allen Stein**: India Habitat Centre. **Hafeez Contractor**: high-rises. | Climate-responsive modernism |
| **Contemporary** | **Lotus Temple** (Delhi, 1986, Fariborz Sahba; Bahá'í House of Worship); **Akshardham** (Delhi, 2005); **Statue of Unity** (2018); **New Parliament Building** (2023, **Bimal Patel / HCP Design**; triangular plan); **Navi Mumbai International Airport** (Zaha Hadid Architects; lotus-inspired; inaugurated 8 Oct 2025) [REPORTED, Vol 11] | — |

## 8. Art Movements (world)

| Movement | Period | Key artists | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Renaissance | 14th–17th c. | Leonardo (*Mona Lisa*, *The Last Supper*), Michelangelo (Sistine Chapel, *David*), Raphael | Linear perspective (Brunelleschi), humanism, realism |
| Baroque | 17th c. | Caravaggio, Rembrandt, Rubens | Drama; **chiaroscuro** (strong light and dark) |
| Romanticism | late 18th–19th c. | Delacroix, Turner, Goya | Emotion, nature's power |
| Realism | mid-19th c. | Courbet, Millet | Everyday life |
| **Impressionism** | 1870s | **Monet** (*Impression, Sunrise* gave the movement its name), Renoir, Degas | Light, outdoor painting (en plein air), visible brushstrokes |
| Post-Impressionism | 1880s–1900s | **Van Gogh** (*The Starry Night*), Cézanne, Gauguin, **Seurat** (pointillism: *A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte*) | Expressive colour, structure |
| Art Nouveau | 1890–1910 | Alphonse Mucha, Gustav Klimt (*The Kiss*) | Organic lines, decorative |
| Expressionism | 1905–1920s | **Edvard Munch** (*The Scream*), Kirchner | Emotional distortion |
| Fauvism | 1905 | **Henri Matisse** | Wild, non-natural colour |
| **Cubism** | 1907–1920s | **Picasso** (*Les Demoiselles d'Avignon*, *Guernica* 1937), **Georges Braque** | Multiple viewpoints, geometric fragmentation |
| Futurism | 1909 (Italy) | Boccioni, Balla | Speed, machines, motion |
| Dada | 1916 (Zurich) | **Marcel Duchamp** (*Fountain*, 1917, a urinal "readymade") | Anti-art, absurdity |
| **Surrealism** | 1924+ | **Salvador Dalí** (*The Persistence of Memory*, melting clocks), René Magritte (*The Treachery of Images*: "Ceci n'est pas une pipe"), Frida Kahlo (associated) | Dreams, the unconscious |
| **De Stijl** | 1917 (Netherlands) | **Piet Mondrian**, Theo van Doesburg, Rietveld | Primary colours + black/white, horizontal and vertical grids |
| **Bauhaus** | 1919–1933 | Gropius, Kandinsky, Klee, Moholy-Nagy, Josef Albers, Marianne Brandt, Anni Albers | Unity of art, craft and technology; "form follows function" applied |
| Constructivism | 1915+ (Russia) | Tatlin, Rodchenko, El Lissitzky | Art for social purpose; graphic posters |
| Art Deco | 1920s–30s | Cassandre (posters), Erté | Geometry, luxury, streamlining |
| Abstract Expressionism | 1940s–50s (NY) | **Jackson Pollock** (drip painting), Mark Rothko (colour fields), de Kooning | Gesture, emotion, large scale |
| **Pop Art** | 1950s–60s | **Andy Warhol** (*Campbell's Soup Cans*, Marilyn), Roy Lichtenstein (comic dots), Richard Hamilton | Consumer culture, mass media |
| Op Art | 1960s | **Bridget Riley**, **Victor Vasarely** | Optical illusions |
| Minimalism | 1960s | Donald Judd, Dan Flavin, Agnes Martin | Simple geometric forms |
| Memphis Design | 1981–88 | Ettore Sottsass | Bold colours, kitsch geometry, anti-modernist |
| Street art | 1980s+ | Banksy, Keith Haring, Jean-Michel Basquiat | Public, political |

**Design movements (summary):**
- **Arts and Crafts** (William Morris, 1860s–1910, UK): a reaction against industrial mass production; handcraft.
- Art Nouveau.
- Deutscher Werkbund (1907).
- Bauhaus.
- Streamlining (1930s USA, Loewy).
- **Mid-century modern** (1945–1970: Eames, Saarinen).
- **Scandinavian design** (functional, natural materials).
- Swiss / International Typographic Style.
- Memphis.
- Postmodern.
- **Flat design** (2010s UI; Windows Metro, iOS 7 in 2013).
- **Skeuomorphism** (UI that imitates real materials: early iOS).
- **Neumorphism** (2020s).
- Apple's **"Liquid Glass"** design language (2025).

## 12. Sustainability, Technology & AI in Design (concepts)

- **Circular design**, **biomaterials** (mycelium packaging, banana-fibre textiles, Piñatex from pineapple leaves), **upcycling**, **zero-waste fashion**, **slow fashion** vs fast fashion.
- **Universal design / accessibility**: the Accessible India Campaign (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan, 2015).
- **Generative AI in design**: text-to-image tools, AI-assisted layouts, the ethics of training data and authorship. **Human-in-the-loop** design keeps humans in charge of judgement, empathy and context.
- **UI/UX**: user interface vs user experience; wireframes; prototypes; user testing.
- **3D printing / additive manufacturing**; **CNC**; **parametric design**; **digital twins**.
- **Smart cities**: India's Smart Cities Mission (2015).
- **Electric mobility**: EV design, charging infrastructure.
