## A. Elements & Principles of Design (1–25)

**DA-1.** A designer wants a poster to feel calm and stable. Which line direction should dominate?
(a) Diagonal (b) Zig-zag (c) Horizontal (d) Spiral
**Ans: (c)**. Horizontal lines echo the horizon and a body at rest, so they read as stability and calm. Diagonals suggest motion; zig-zags suggest tension. *Concept:* line psychology · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* tests basic visual vocabulary · *Mistake:* choosing vertical, which signals strength or height rather than calm.

**DA-2.** Which element of design is defined as "the lightness or darkness of a colour or tone"?
(a) Hue (b) Value (c) Saturation (d) Texture
**Ans: (b)**. Value is lightness or darkness. Hue is the colour family; saturation is intensity. *Concept:* elements · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* terminology precision · *Mistake:* confusing value with saturation.

**DA-3.** The difference between "shape" and "form" is that:
(a) shape is 3D, form is 2D (b) shape is 2D, form is 3D (c) both are 3D (d) shape refers only to geometric figures
**Ans: (b)**. A circle is a shape; a sphere is a form. *Concept:* elements · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* the basis for product sketching · *Mistake:* using the words interchangeably.

**DA-4.** A temple corridor with pillars at equal intervals creates mainly:
(a) Radial balance (b) Regular rhythm (c) Emphasis (d) Variety
**Ans: (b)**. Equal repetition at equal intervals produces regular rhythm. *Concept:* rhythm · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* Indian architectural context · *Mistake:* answering "repetition", which is not offered here; rhythm is repetition with tempo.

**DA-5.** In a layout, a large pale-grey circle on the left is balanced by a small bright-red square on the right. This is:
(a) Symmetrical balance (b) Asymmetrical balance (c) Radial balance (d) Imbalance
**Ans: (b)**. Different elements of equal visual weight balance each other: a high-saturation small shape against a low-saturation large one. *Concept:* visual weight · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* composition judgement · *Mistake:* thinking that different sizes must mean imbalance.

**DA-6.** Which principle is MOST concerned with the order in which a viewer reads information?
(a) Unity (b) Hierarchy (c) Harmony (d) Proportion
**Ans: (b)**. Hierarchy arranges elements by importance through size, weight and position. *Concept:* hierarchy · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* poster and signage tasks · *Mistake:* choosing emphasis, which concerns a single focal point rather than an ordered sequence.

**DA-7.** A rangoli drawn around a central lamp, with motifs repeating outward, best demonstrates:
(a) Asymmetry (b) Radial balance (c) Progression (d) Contrast
**Ans: (b)**. Elements radiate from a centre. *Concept:* balance types · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* Indian visual culture · *Mistake:* choosing symmetry in general rather than its radial kind.

**DA-8.** A comic artist draws a hero much larger than surrounding buildings to show power. This uses:
(a) Proportion (b) Scale (exaggerated) (c) Texture (d) Rhythm
**Ans: (b)**. Scale is size relative to surroundings or the human; exaggerating it creates drama. *Concept:* scale vs proportion · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* imagination drawings · *Mistake:* choosing proportion, which compares parts within one object.

**DA-9.** Which pair BEST describes the risk of too much variety and too much unity respectively?
(a) Boredom, chaos (b) Chaos, boredom (c) Harmony, contrast (d) Emphasis, rhythm
**Ans: (b)**. Too much variety creates chaos; too much unity creates monotony. *Concept:* unity–variety balance · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* aesthetic judgement · *Mistake:* reversing the pair.

**DA-10.** Seurat's paintings are made entirely of tiny dots. Which element is the building block?
(a) Line (b) Point (c) Plane (d) Space
**Ans: (b)**. Pointillism builds tone and colour from points. *Concept:* point · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* links art history and elements · *Mistake:* answering "colour", which the dots carry but which is not the structural unit.

**DA-11.** "Visual texture" is best illustrated by:
(a) a jute bag you can touch (b) a drawing of tree bark on smooth paper (c) a ceramic tile's glaze (d) sandpaper
**Ans: (b)**. The texture is implied visually, not felt. *Concept:* texture · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* rendering skill · *Mistake:* picking a real material.

**DA-12.** An emphasis strategy that does NOT rely on colour is:
(a) red text on grey (b) isolation of one object in empty space (c) a yellow highlight (d) a complementary accent
**Ans: (b)**. Isolation creates a focal point through space alone. *Concept:* emphasis methods · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* monochrome tasks · *Mistake:* assuming emphasis always needs colour.

**DA-13.** Which arrangement shows *progressive* rhythm?
(a) ○ ○ ○ ○ (b) ○ □ ○ □ (c) ∘ ○ ◯ ⬤ (increasing size) (d) random dots
**Ans: (c)**. A gradual, step-by-step change is progressive rhythm. (a) is regular; (b) is alternating. *Concept:* rhythm types · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* pattern logic · *Mistake:* choosing (b).

**DA-14.** Negative space refers to:
(a) dark areas (b) the empty area around and between subjects (c) shadows (d) unused paper only at margins
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* space · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* logo cleverness (FedEx, WWF) · *Mistake:* equating it with darkness.

**DA-15.** Which composition has a single dominant focal point?
(a) Two identical faces of the same size at the left and right edges (b) One red umbrella in a crowd of black umbrellas (c) A grid of 16 equal photos (d) A uniform wallpaper pattern
**Ans: (b)**. Contrast in colour isolates one element. *Concept:* emphasis · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* the classic visual example · *Mistake:* none common.

**DA-16.** A designer repeats the same blue, the same font, and aligns everything to a grid across 10 brochure pages. The main principle achieved is:
(a) Variety (b) Unity (c) Contrast (d) Movement
**Ans: (b)**. Consistency creates unity. *Concept:* unity · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* brand systems · *Mistake:* choosing harmony; unity is the better term for consistency across a system.

**DA-17.** In Indian miniature paintings, important figures are often painted larger regardless of distance. This convention is called:
(a) Linear perspective (b) Hierarchical (hieratic) scale (c) Foreshortening (d) Atmospheric perspective
**Ans: (b)**. Size shows importance rather than distance. *Concept:* scale conventions · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* Indian art awareness · *Mistake:* choosing foreshortening.

**DA-18.** Which line type best expresses "anger" in an abstract drawing?
(a) Thin wavy lines (b) Heavy jagged lines with sharp angles (c) Long horizontals (d) Gentle spirals
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* expressive line · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* "express an emotion using only lines" tasks · *Mistake:* choosing spirals, which suggest confusion or hypnosis.

**DA-19.** The golden ratio is approximately:
(a) 1 : 1.414 (b) 1 : 1.618 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 1 : 3.14
**Ans: (b)**. 1.414 is the A-paper ratio (√2). *Concept:* proportion · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* proportion knowledge · *Mistake:* choosing 1.414.

**DA-20.** Which BEST describes "movement" as a design principle?
(a) Objects that physically move (b) The path the viewer's eye follows through a composition (c) Animation only (d) Diagonal lines only
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* movement · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* reading posters · *Mistake:* taking it literally.

**DA-21.** A logo is made of only three vertical rectangles whose heights increase from left to right, like a rising bar chart. What does the arrangement most likely communicate?
(a) Decline (b) Growth or progress (c) Stability (d) Chaos
**Ans: (b)**. For left-to-right readers, an ascending progression reads as growth. This is a common trope in finance and education logos. *Concept:* visual semantics · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* symbol interpretation · *Mistake:* choosing "stability" because the shapes are rectangles.

**DA-22.** Which principle is violated when a poster has a headline, image, date and logo all of the same size and weight?
(a) Unity (b) Hierarchy (c) Repetition (d) Balance
**Ans: (b)**. Nothing tells the viewer what to read first. *Concept:* hierarchy · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* critique skills · *Mistake:* choosing balance.

**DA-23.** Which statement about "harmony" is correct?
(a) It requires identical elements (b) It arises when elements are compatible, e.g. analogous colours and similar shapes (c) It needs high contrast (d) It means symmetry
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* harmony · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* aesthetic vocabulary · *Mistake:* confusing it with symmetry.

**DA-24.** A designer places the horizon exactly through the middle of a landscape. A tutor would most likely suggest:
(a) Keep it centred for drama (b) Move it to a third line to give the sky or ground dominance (c) Remove the horizon (d) Add a second horizon
**Ans: (b)**. A centred horizon splits the image into two competing halves; the rule of thirds creates dominance. *Concept:* composition · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* scene drawing · *Mistake:* choosing (d), which breaks perspective.

**DA-25.** Which is an example of "contrast of texture"?
(a) Rough jute placemat beside a glossy steel plate (b) Two shades of blue (c) A big and a small circle (d) Black text on white
**Ans: (a)**. The others are contrasts of hue, size and value. *Concept:* contrast types · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* material awareness · *Mistake:* choosing (d).

## B. Colour (26–45)

**DA-26.** On the RYB colour wheel, the complement of yellow is:
(a) Blue (b) Violet (c) Green (d) Orange
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* complements · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* standard item · *Mistake:* choosing blue (in RGB terms the complement of yellow is blue; NID-level questions assume the painter's RYB wheel).

**DA-27.** Mixing equal amounts of cyan and magenta ink gives approximately:
(a) Green (b) Blue / violet-blue (c) Red (d) Black
**Ans: (b)**. In CMY subtractive mixing, C + M = blue, M + Y = red, C + Y = green. *Concept:* subtractive colour · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* print awareness · *Mistake:* confusing it with RGB.

**DA-28.** Which colour model do mobile screens use?
(a) CMYK (b) RYB (c) RGB (d) Pantone
**Ans: (c)**. Screens emit light (additive). *Concept:* colour models · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* digital design awareness · *Mistake:* choosing CMYK.

**DA-29.** A "tone" is produced by adding to a hue:
(a) White (b) Black (c) Grey (d) Its complement only
**Ans: (c)**. A tint adds white, a shade adds black, a tone adds grey. *Concept:* tint/shade/tone · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* frequently confused trio · *Mistake:* choosing black.

**DA-30.** A palette of yellow-green, green and blue-green is:
(a) Complementary (b) Analogous (c) Triadic (d) Split-complementary
**Ans: (b)**. These are neighbours on the wheel. *Concept:* schemes · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* colour application · *Mistake:* choosing monochromatic, which uses only one hue.

**DA-31.** Which set is triadic on the RYB wheel?
(a) Red, orange, yellow (b) Orange, green, violet (c) Blue, orange, white (d) Red, red-violet, violet
**Ans: (b)**. The secondaries are 120° apart. *Concept:* triadic · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* scheme identification · *Mistake:* choosing (a), which is analogous.

**DA-32.** Why do many food brands use red and yellow?
(a) They are cool colours (b) Warm colours are associated with energy, appetite and quick attention (c) They are cheapest to print (d) They recede visually
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* colour psychology · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* branding awareness · *Mistake:* choosing (d); warm colours advance.

**DA-33.** In a landscape painting, which technique makes mountains appear far away?
(a) Brighter, warmer, more detailed (b) Paler, bluer, less detailed (c) Thicker outlines (d) Higher saturation
**Ans: (b)**. This is atmospheric (aerial) perspective. *Concept:* depth through colour · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* scene drawing · *Mistake:* choosing (d).

**DA-34.** Red text on a green background of equal brightness is a poor choice mainly because:
(a) the colours are analogous (b) the value contrast is low and red–green colour-blind viewers cannot separate them (c) red is too warm (d) green is unlucky
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* accessibility, value contrast · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* inclusive design · *Mistake:* choosing (a); red and green are complementary, not analogous.

**DA-35.** The "60-30-10 rule" refers to:
(a) Image : text : white-space ratio (b) Dominant : secondary : accent colour proportions (c) Font sizes (d) Margins
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* colour proportion · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* interior and poster colouring · *Mistake:* none.

**DA-36.** In India, which colour is traditionally associated with mourning in many communities (but with purity in others)?
(a) Red (b) White (c) Yellow (d) Green
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* cultural colour meaning · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* cultural sensitivity in design · *Mistake:* assuming the Western black-for-mourning convention applies.

**DA-37.** Which colour combination gives the highest legibility for a road sign seen at night with headlights?
(a) Yellow on white (b) White (retroreflective) on dark green or blue (c) Light grey on white (d) Pink on red
**Ans: (b)**. High value contrast plus retroreflective sheeting. *Concept:* legibility · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* signage tasks · *Mistake:* none.

**DA-38.** Adding white to blue produces:
(a) Navy (shade) (b) Sky blue (tint) (c) Grey-blue (tone) (d) Violet
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* tint · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* terminology · *Mistake:* answering "tone".

**DA-39.** Saturation describes:
(a) how light or dark a colour is (b) the purity or intensity of a colour (c) the colour family (d) its temperature
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* colour attributes · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* terminology · *Mistake:* answering value.

**DA-40.** A split-complementary scheme based on red uses red plus:
(a) green (b) yellow-green and blue-green (c) orange and violet (d) blue and yellow
**Ans: (b)**. The complement of red is green; its two neighbours are yellow-green and blue-green. *Concept:* schemes · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* applied colour · *Mistake:* choosing (d), which is triadic.

**DA-41.** Which colour is commonly used for "mandatory action" signs (e.g. "wear helmet") in the ISO safety system?
(a) Red (b) Yellow (c) Blue (d) Green
**Ans: (c)**. Red = prohibition, yellow = warning, blue = mandatory, green = safe condition / exit. *Concept:* safety colours · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* signage design · *Mistake:* choosing red.

**DA-42.** Mixing all three RYB paint primaries in equal amounts gives roughly:
(a) White (b) A dark brown or grey (c) Bright green (d) Pink
**Ans: (b)**. Subtractive mixing absorbs most light. *Concept:* pigment mixing · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* hands-on colouring · *Mistake:* choosing white, which is the result for light.

**DA-43.** A monochromatic poster using only shades of saffron would feel:
(a) chaotic (b) unified, warm and calm (c) cold (d) highly contrasted in hue
**Ans: (b)**. One hue gives unity; saffron is warm. *Concept:* monochrome · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* constraint tasks · *Mistake:* choosing (d).

**DA-44.** Which colour pair is complementary?
(a) Blue–green (b) Orange–blue (c) Yellow–orange (d) Red–violet
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* complements · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* basic · *Mistake:* none.

**DA-45.** A hospital ward is painted pale green or pale blue rather than bright red mainly because:
(a) red paint is expensive (b) cool, low-saturation colours are calming and reduce visual fatigue (c) green is a primary colour (d) it hides dirt
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* colour psychology in spaces · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* environment design · *Mistake:* (d) is a minor side benefit, not the main reason.

## C. Typography & Visual Communication (46–65)

**DA-46.** Adjusting space uniformly across an entire word is called:
