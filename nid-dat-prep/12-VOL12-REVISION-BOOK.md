## 1. ONE-PAGE EXAM STRATEGY (read this the night before)
- **Exam:** Sunday 20 Dec 2026 · 3 hrs · 100 marks · pen and paper · no negative marking · Prelims = **shortlisting only**.
- **Two possible formats:** (A) 22 objective + 4 subjective (the Handbook scheme); (B) all-subjective, about 6 questions with sub-parts (reported for 2026). Adapt within the first 5 minutes.
- **First 5–10 minutes:** read everything; **circle the counts, viewpoints, users and colour limits**; write a time budget next to each question; jot 2 idea keywords per question.
- **Time rule:** minutes ≈ marks × 1.65. Use the **70% rule**: move on when a drawing is 70% done.
- **Never leave a question blank.** A 3-minute labelled sketch scores marks; a blank scores zero.
- **The order of value:** idea → clarity → composition → proportion → rendering.
- **MCQs:** answer all of them (no negative marking). Skip anything over 90 seconds, then come back.
- **Last 15 minutes:** fill unanswered MCQs, add labels and titles, add colour to the strongest answer, check the counts.

## 2. VISUAL & DESIGN PRINCIPLES (core list)
- **Elements:** point, line, shape (2D), form (3D), colour, texture (actual or visual), space (positive or negative), value.
- **Principles:** balance (symmetrical / asymmetrical / radial), contrast, emphasis, hierarchy, proportion (part to part), scale (relative to the human or another object), rhythm (regular / alternating / progressive / flowing), repetition, movement (the eye path), unity, variety, harmony.
- **Gestalt:** proximity, similarity, closure, continuity, figure–ground, common fate, symmetry, prägnanz.
- **Composition:**
  - the rule of thirds
  - one focal point
  - odd numbers of subjects
  - lead room in front of a moving subject
  - leading lines
  - framing
  - foreground, middle ground and background
  - avoid tangents
  - don't put the horizon in the middle
- **Design thinking (Stanford):** Empathise → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test. **Double Diamond:** Discover → Define → Develop → Deliver.
- **Don Norman:** affordance, signifier, feedback, mapping, constraint. **Dieter Rams:** "Less, but better", 10 principles.
- **Universal design:** works for the widest range of users (the curb-cut effect). **Ergonomics:** design for the 5th–95th percentile.
- **Sustainability:** refuse, reduce, reuse, repair, recycle, rot; circular economy; cradle to cradle; design for disassembly; mono-material packaging; avoid greenwashing.

## 3. COLOUR (essentials)
- **RYB primaries:** red, yellow, blue → secondaries orange, green, violet → tertiaries such as red-orange.
- **RGB (light, additive):** R+G = yellow, G+B = cyan, R+B = magenta, all three = white.
- **CMYK (print, subtractive):** C+M = blue, M+Y = red, C+Y = green.
- **Complements (RYB):** red–green, blue–orange, yellow–violet.
- **Tint** = + white; **shade** = + black; **tone** = + grey.
- **Hue** = the colour family; **saturation** = intensity; **value** = lightness.
- **Schemes:** monochromatic, analogous, complementary, split-complementary, triadic, tetradic.
- **60-30-10 rule.** Warm colours advance and cool colours recede. **Atmospheric perspective:** distant objects look paler and bluer.
- **Accessibility:** about 8% of men are red–green colour-blind. Use value contrast plus shape, never colour alone.
- **Safety colours:** red = prohibition, yellow = warning, blue = mandatory, green = safe or exit.
- **India:** saffron = sacred and courage; white = mourning (in many communities); red = bridal and auspicious.

## 4. TYPOGRAPHY & VISUAL COMMUNICATION
- **Serif** (Times, Garamond, Bodoni, Baskerville) · **Sans** (Helvetica, Futura, Gill Sans, Arial, Univers, Frutiger) · **Slab** (Rockwell) · Script · Display · Monospace.
- **Kerning** = one letter pair · **Tracking** = a whole word or line · **Leading** = line spacing · **Counter** = the enclosed space in a letter · **x-height** · baseline · ascender and descender.
- **Legibility** = telling characters apart (a property of the typeface); **readability** = reading blocks of text easily (a property of the layout).
- **Rules:** at most 2 typefaces; 45–75 characters per line; use sans serif with high contrast for signage.
- **Pictograms:** a simple silhouette, a consistent stroke and grid, one idea per sign, no text needed, a red circle-and-slash for prohibition.
- **Poster:** headline (3–6 words) → one strong image → supporting line → call to action → logo, date and venue.
- **Logo types:** wordmark, lettermark, pictorial, abstract, mascot, emblem, combination. A good logo is simple, memorable, scalable, works in mono, and is appropriate.
- **Packaging functions:** protect, preserve, contain, communicate, convenience, sustainability.

## 5. PERSPECTIVE & DRAWING RULES
- **HL** = eye level. **1-point** (frontal: roads, rooms) · **2-point** (corners; verticals stay vertical) · **3-point** (looking up or down).
- **Standing viewer:** the heads of all standing adults sit on the HL, at any distance. A child half an adult's height has its head **halfway between its feet and the HL**.
- **Bird's-eye** = a high horizon with a third VP below; **worm's-eye** = a low horizon with a third VP above.
- **Human proportions:** an adult is 7.5–8 heads tall; the midpoint is the hips; the fingertips reach mid-thigh; the eyes sit at half the head's height.
- **Size references:**

| Object | Approximate size |
|---|---|
| Door | 2.1 m |
| Adult | 1.6–1.75 m |
| Chair seat | 0.45 m |
| Table | 0.75 m |
| Kitchen counter | 0.85 m |
| Bus | 3 m tall |
| Auto-rickshaw | 1.7 m tall |

- **Shadows:** fall away from the light; are long in the morning and evening and shortest at noon; morning shadows point west.
- **Materials:** glass = sharp highlights and see-through; steel = high contrast with reflections; wood = grain; fabric = folds from tension points.
- **Storyboards:** plan the beats; keep characters and setting consistent; vary wide, medium and close shots; follow the 180° rule; number the frames; make the last frame show the resolution or a design improvement.
- **Product sketch:** a 3/4 view + 1–2 detail views + labels + a hand for scale + a small in-use scene + a 2-line justification.

## 6. REASONING FORMULAS & RULES
- **Mirror clock:** 11:60 − time (use 23:60 − time for times from 11:00 to 12:59).
- **Vertically symmetric letters:** A H I M O T U V W X Y. **Horizontally symmetric letters:** B C D E H I K O X. **Both:** H I O X.
- **Letters that look the same after a 180° rotation:** H I N O S X Z.
- **Folding:** n folds → 2ⁿ layers and holes. Unfold in **reverse** order. A fold along y = x swaps the coordinates; a fold along x + y = s gives (s − y, s − x).
- **Painted cube (n):** 8 corners (3 faces); 12(n−2) edges (2 faces); 6(n−2)² face-centres (1 face); (n−2)³ inside (0 faces).
- **Dice:** opposite faces sum to 7. **Nets:** in a row, faces one apart are opposite; in a 1-4-1 net, the square above and the square below are opposite. A cube has **11** nets.
- **Euler:** V − E + F = 2.
- **Counting:**
  - squares in an n×n grid = Σk²; in a 4×4 grid = 30; in a 5×5 grid = 55
  - rectangles in an m×n grid = C(m+1, 2) × C(n+1, 2)
  - triangles from a vertex with k lines and b bases = C(k, 2) × b
  - a square with both diagonals = 8 triangles; adding both midlines gives 16
- **Gears:** meshing gears alternate direction; teeth × turns is constant; an odd closed loop jams. An open belt keeps the direction; a crossed belt reverses it.
- **Clock angle:** |30H − 5.5M|. Handshakes: n(n−1)/2. Diagonals of a polygon: n(n−3)/2.
- **Interior angle sum:** (n−2) × 180°. Only triangles, squares and hexagons tessellate alone.
- **Scaling:** lengths × k → areas × k² → volumes × k³.
- **A-paper:** A0 = 1 m²; the sides are in the ratio 1 : √2; 1 A1 = 8 A4. **Golden ratio** ≈ 1.618.
- **Percentages:** successive changes a and b give a + b + ab/100; +20% then −20% = −4%. Profit % is on cost.
- **Speed:** km/h × 5/18 = m/s. Average speed over equal distances = 2ab/(a + b).

## 13. TOP 100 NID DAT MISTAKES STUDENTS MAKE (and the fix for each)

### A. Time management (1–12)
1. Spending 40+ minutes on the first drawing. **Fix:** write a time budget per question before starting.
2. Polishing one drawing and leaving the last question blank. **Fix:** use the 70% rule.
3. Not reading the whole paper first. **Fix:** spend 5 minutes reading everything.
4. Getting stuck on a hard MCQ. **Fix:** move on after 90 seconds.
5. No buffer time. **Fix:** keep 15–20 minutes for finishing touches.
6. Starting to colour before all the questions are drafted. **Fix:** colour last.
7. Not wearing a watch (where allowed). **Fix:** practise with the same watch.
8. Not practising full 3-hour papers. **Fix:** at least 5 timed mocks.
9. Erasing too much. **Fix:** start with light construction lines, then commit.
10. Rewriting text answers. **Fix:** draft mentally, then write once.
11. Underestimating multi-part questions. **Fix:** count the sub-tasks and split the time.
12. Panicking after one bad question. **Fix:** it's one of 14+ tasks; reset and move on.

### B. Reading instructions (13–22)
13. Drawing 3 items when 5 are asked for. **Fix:** circle the numbers.
14. Ignoring the viewpoint (e.g. "from a crow's view"). **Fix:** underline the viewpoint words.
15. Using extra colours despite a limit. **Fix:** put the allowed colours in a separate pile.
16. Exceeding or falling short of the word limit. **Fix:** count the lines (about 10–12 words per line).
17. Missing "label" or "explain" instructions. **Fix:** tick each instruction when done.
18. Answering a different question (e.g. drawing a product when a poster was asked for). **Fix:** restate the task in the margin.
19. Ignoring the specified user (elderly, child). **Fix:** draw the user in the solution.
20. Drawing outside the box or margins. **Fix:** plan the layout first.
21. Not numbering the frames. **Fix:** always number them.
22. Forgetting the title or caption when asked. **Fix:** add a checklist line at the end.

### C. Drawing & proportion (23–40)
23. People taller than doors. **Fix:** use size references.
24. Floating people. **Fix:** anchor their feet on the ground plane; add cast shadows.
25. Several horizon lines in one scene. **Fix:** one HL, drawn first.
26. Tilted verticals in 1- and 2-point perspective. **Fix:** use a scale for the verticals.
27. VPs too close together. **Fix:** put the VPs far apart, even off the page.
28. Stiff, mannequin figures. **Fix:** start with the line of action and gesture.
29. Identical faces and poses for every person. **Fix:** vary age, posture and clothing.
30. Tiny drawings in huge boxes. **Fix:** fill about 70–80% of the box.
31. Over-detailing one corner. **Fix:** spread the detail across the focal hierarchy.
32. Shading with no consistent light source. **Fix:** draw a small sun or arrow first.
33. Messy, hairy lines. **Fix:** ghost the stroke, then draw it confidently.
34. Not showing materials. **Fix:** use texture conventions for each material.
35. Hands hidden behind backs to avoid drawing them. **Fix:** practise mitten hands.
36. Ignoring depth layers. **Fix:** foreground, middle and background.
37. Wrong object details (e.g. a bicycle chain on the left). **Fix:** do weekly memory-vs-reality checks.
38. Drawing from imagination only, never from observation. **Fix:** daily sketching from life.
39. Perspective on circles (ellipses) going wrong. **Fix:** practise ellipses in boxes.
40. Rendering photorealistically at the cost of the idea. **Fix:** idea first.

### D. Composition & communication (41–55)
41. Centring everything. **Fix:** use the rule of thirds.
42. No focal point. **Fix:** create one with contrast, size or isolation.
43. Clutter. **Fix:** remove elements that don't serve the story.
44. Tangents. **Fix:** overlap the objects or separate them clearly.
45. Poor hierarchy in posters. **Fix:** headline > image > text > logo.
46. Too much text on a poster or sign. **Fix:** 3–6-word headlines; use icons.
47. Illegible handwriting. **Fix:** print in capitals for labels.
48. Labels with no arrows. **Fix:** use thin leader lines.
49. Unclear storyboards. **Fix:** vary the shots; keep the characters consistent.
50. Changing the character's look between frames. **Fix:** give them one distinctive accessory.
51. A story with no resolution. **Fix:** plan the beats: setup, problem, action, resolution.
52. Ignoring negative space. **Fix:** let the subject breathe.
53. Random colours. **Fix:** choose a scheme (analogous, complementary).
54. Low contrast in signs. **Fix:** dark on light or light on dark.
55. A logo that fails in black and white. **Fix:** test a mono version.

### E. Creativity & originality (56–68)
56. The first cliché (a crying Earth, a melting globe). **Fix:** generate 5 ideas and pick the 3rd or 4th.
57. Copying a famous logo or poster from memory. **Fix:** start from the brief's keywords.
58. Being "creative" but irrelevant. **Fix:** check the idea against the brief.
59. Only one category of ideas (low flexibility). **Fix:** vary the categories (tool, toy, art, shelter).
60. Overusing technology (apps, drones, robots). **Fix:** think physical, low-cost and contextual first.
61. Unrealistic magic solutions. **Fix:** hint at the mechanism or material.
62. Fear of simple ideas. **Fix:** a simple idea done brilliantly beats a complex mess.
63. Not using the Indian context. **Fix:** add local details (chai, autos, rangoli).
64. Ignoring humour or emotion where appropriate. **Fix:** a touch of wit makes answers memorable.
65. Not personalising "product you love" answers. **Fix:** a real story plus specific details.
66. Repeating the same idea style in every answer. **Fix:** vary your approaches.
67. Ignoring viewpoint as a creative lever. **Fix:** try unusual angles.
68. Not sketching thumbnails. **Fix:** 3 thumbnails in 3 minutes.

### F. Problem-solving & user focus (69–80)
69. Jumping to a solution without defining the problem. **Fix:** write a one-line problem statement.
70. Designing for yourself, not the user. **Fix:** draw the user.
71. Ignoring constraints (cost, electricity, water). **Fix:** list the constraints first.
72. Solving one stakeholder's problem only (e.g. in the neem-branch dispute). **Fix:** consider every party.
73. Destructive solutions (ban it, cut it down). **Fix:** look for win-win, sustainable fixes.
74. Ignoring safety, especially for children's products. **Fix:** add one safety note.
75. Ignoring maintenance and cleaning. **Fix:** think about the whole life cycle.
76. Ignoring accessibility. **Fix:** consider low vision, mobility and literacy.
77. No explanation of how the solution works. **Fix:** labels plus a 2-line note.
78. No in-use scene. **Fix:** a small inset showing the user using it.
79. Over-complex systems for a small problem. **Fix:** proportionate solutions.
80. Not considering the systemic fix (e.g. repairing the pothole, not just helping the person who fell). **Fix:** end with the system improvement.

### G. Visual reasoning & objective questions (81–92)
81. Forgetting to reverse the letter order in mirror words. **Fix:** reverse, then flip each letter.
82. Confusing a mirror image with a rotation. **Fix:** a rotation never produces a mirror image.
83. Unfolding in the wrong order. **Fix:** unfold in reverse.
84. Reflecting across the wrong axis in folding. **Fix:** draw the fold line.
85. Calling faces opposite when they are adjacent in nets. **Fix:** the one-apart rule.
86. Forgetting the downward triangles when counting. **Fix:** count systematically by size and orientation.
87. Mixing up the RGB and RYB wheels. **Fix:** check whether the question is about paint or light.
88. Overthinking simple GK. **Fix:** go with the first well-founded answer.
89. Leaving MCQs blank (there is no negative marking). **Fix:** guess intelligently.
90. Unit errors (cm vs m). **Fix:** convert first.
91. Accepting a syllogism conclusion that is only possible. **Fix:** it must be true in every diagram.
92. Careless option marking. **Fix:** re-check the question number against the answer sheet.

### H. General awareness & preparation habits (93–100)
93. Memorising GK without visuals. **Fix:** look at images of every craft and building you learn.
94. Ignoring Indian crafts and textiles. **Fix:** 3 crafts a day from Vol 2C.
95. Cramming current affairs. **Fix:** link each event to a design idea.
96. Not keeping an error log. **Fix:** Vol 13 §6, updated daily.
97. Practising only favourite topics. **Fix:** follow the plan's rotation.
98. Not getting feedback on drawings. **Fix:** a weekly review by a teacher or design student.
99. Skipping sleep before the exam. **Fix:** 7–8 hours; no all-nighters.
100. Forgetting the admit card, ID or kit. **Fix:** pack the night before using the checklist.
