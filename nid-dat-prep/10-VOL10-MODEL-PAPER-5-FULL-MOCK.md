## DETAILED SOLUTIONS — PART I

**Q1 → (b) Kishangarh.** Painted by Nihal Chand, 18th century. *Diff:* M.

**Q2 → (a) 1:35.** 11:60 − 10:25 = 1:35. *Check:* the minute hand at "5" (25 min) mirrors to "7" (35 min); the hour hand at about 10.4 mirrors to about 1.6, i.e. between 1 and 2 ✓. *Diff:* M.

**Q3 → (b) 3.** D, E and I are symmetric about a horizontal axis. S is not (it has rotational symmetry only); G and N are not. *Diff:* M.

**Q4 → (b) Rajaraja Chola I** (c. 1010 CE). Rajendra Chola I built Gangaikonda Cholapuram. *Diff:* M.

**Q5 → (b) Johnston**, by Edward Johnston. Gill Sans (1928) was designed by his former student Eric Gill. *Diff:* M.

**Q6 → (b) neutral greyish-brown.** Complementary pigments neutralise each other. *Diff:* M.

**Q7 → (d) Gae Aulenti.** The other three are Pritzker laureates (Hadid 2004, Sejima 2010 with Nishizawa, Lacaton 2021 with Vassal). Aulenti was a distinguished Italian architect and designer (the Musée d'Orsay conversion) but did not win the Pritzker. *Diff:* H.

**Q8 → (b) 35.** n(n − 3)/2 = 10 × 7 / 2 = 35. *Diff:* M.

**Q9 → (c) 56.** The differences are 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, so the next is +14: 42 + 14 = 56. (The terms follow n(n + 1).) *Diff:* E.

**Q10 → (b) Moidams of Charaideo** (Assam, 2024). Santiniketan was inscribed in 2023; Ramappa and Dholavira in 2021. *Diff:* M.

**Q11 → (b) Leonardo da Vinci.** Each is a proportion study by its creator. Vitruvius wrote the text that Leonardo illustrated, but the drawing is Leonardo's. *Diff:* M.

**Q12 → (c) 9 m.** At the same time and place, shadow length is proportional to height: a 3 : 2 ratio, so 6 × 1.5 = 9. *Diff:* E.

**Q13 → (b).** The geometry physically prevents a wrong insertion. *Diff:* E.

**Q14 → (a) indigo and madder red.** *Diff:* M.

**Q15 → (c) 40.** 1×1 squares: 4 × 5 = 20. 2×2: 3 × 4 = 12. 3×3: 2 × 3 = 6. 4×4: 1 × 2 = 2. Total 40. *Diff:* M.

**Q16 → (b) 4.** Fold with face 3 as the base:
- Face 2 (above 3 in the net) becomes the north wall.
- Face 1 (left of 2) folds down to become the west wall.
- Face 4 (right of 3) becomes the east wall.
- Face 5 (below 4) folds around the south-east corner edge to become the south wall.
- Face 6 (right of 5) folds over to become the top.

So the opposite pairs are 3–6, 2–5 and **1–4**. *Diff:* H. (The 2-2-2 "staircase" is one of the 11 valid cube nets.)

**Q17 → (c) √5 ≈ 2.24 m.** Unfold two adjacent faces into a flat 2 × 1 rectangle; the shortest path is its diagonal, √(2² + 1²) = √5. Option (b), √3, is the straight line *through* the cube, which an ant on the surface cannot take. Walking along three edges gives 3 m. *Diff:* H.

**Q18 → (d) Lata.** Jai = chair and Kavi = clock. Ira cannot have the lamp or the clock, so Ira = kettle. That leaves Lata = lamp. *Diff:* M.

**Q19 → (d) 4,000 L.** Volume scales by 20³ = 8,000, and 0.5 × 8,000 = 4,000 L. *Diff:* M.

**Q20 → (b).** It is a visual nudge at the exact point of action, separating the two flows. *Diff:* M.

**Q21 → (b) Only II.** Glazed objects are fired (they are among "some fired objects") and are not porous, so some fired objects are not porous (II ✓). We cannot tell whether any glazed fired objects are ceramics, so I is not certain. *Diff:* H.

**Q22 → (c).** At a given distance, height scales uniformly between the ground point and the eye level. An adult's head reaches the horizon (the viewer's eye height), so a half-height child's head reaches halfway between the ground contact point and the horizon. *Diff:* H. (This is a very useful rule for drawing crowds of mixed ages.)

## DETAILED SOLUTIONS — PART II (model approaches + rubrics)

### Q23. Monobloc chair critique & redesign (15)

**(a) Memory drawing (5):**
- a one-piece moulded form
- a backrest with horizontal slots or a fan pattern
- splayed legs, wider at the bottom, which allows stacking
- curved armrests flowing into the seat
- a slight seat dip
- moulding ribs under the seat (an observation bonus)

Rubric: form accuracy 2, proportion 1, details 1, line quality 1.

**(b) Strengths and weaknesses (4).**
- *Strengths:* cheap; lightweight; stackable; weatherproof and easy to clean; made in one moulding.
- *Weaknesses:* legs can crack or splay under load (safety); it gets hot in the sun; it is not ergonomic for long sitting; it is not easily recyclable once it has UV damage; it looks visually monotonous; it slides on smooth floors.

Rubric: 4 valid points minimum (about 0.67 each, rounded).

**(c) Redesign for a primary classroom (6), model: "Grow-Seat"**
- Sized for children aged 6–10: seat height 32–35 cm.
- **Reversible base:** flipped one way it gives a 32 cm seat; flipped the other it gives 36 cm, so it grows with the child.
- A **rounded-edge** recycled-plastic shell with **non-slip rubber feet**; a **hook at the back** for the school bag; a **cut-out handle** in the backrest so children can lift and carry it easily.
- **Rocking prevention:** wide-stance feet. **Stackable** to clear the floor for activities.
- **Colours:** muted primaries that can be colour-coded by group.

Rubric: child ergonomics 2, classroom-specific features 2, drawing and labels 2.

### Q24. One street corner at three times (20)

**Plan.** Fixed viewpoint: a 2-point perspective of a corner with a tea stall, a medical shop, a bus stop, a tree, a streetlight and a temple gate.

| | 6 am | 1 pm | 9 pm |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Light** | Low warm sun from the east; long shadows pointing west; mist | Harsh overhead sun; short, dark shadows under objects | Artificial light: streetlight cones, shop glow, headlights; deep blue sky |
| **Activities** | Milkman, newspaper boy, morning walkers, the tea stall opening, temple bells | Office crowd at the stall, a delivery rider sheltering under the tree, a vendor's umbrella, few walkers (heat) | Families eating street food, the medical shop busy, a security guard, a dog sleeping, shutters half-down |
| **Mood / colours** | Fresh pale yellows and pinks | Bleached, high contrast, hot | Warm pools of light against a cool dark |

**Execution tips.**
- Lightly trace the same construction lines in each frame (or draw the outlines with a scale for consistency).
- Change only the light, the people and the props.
- Shadow direction must follow the sun: pointing west in the morning, short at noon, and spreading out from the lamps at night.

**Rubric (20).**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Identical viewpoint and consistency | 4 |
| Light logic (sun angle, shadows, artificial light) | 5 |
| Activity and user change: observation richness | 5 |
| Mood via colour / value | 3 |
| Drawing quality | 3 |

**Mistakes:** the viewpoint drifts between frames; the same people appear in all three frames; ignoring shadows.

### Q25. "Kala Setu" identity (15)

**(a) Logo (7), model concept:** *setu* means "bridge". Draw an **arch bridge** formed by two hands: one decorated with a mehendi-like craft pattern (the artisan), the other plain with a shopping-bag strap (the buyer). Their fingertips meet at the keystone. Beneath the arch, three short lines suggest a river, which double as a loom's warp threads. The wordmark "KALA SETU" is set in a warm humanist sans, with a small Devanagari "कला सेतु" sub-line. Colours: terracotta (craft, earth) + indigo (trust, textile).

**(b) Applications (4):**
- *Shopping bag:* a jute or kraft bag with the logo printed large in indigo; a strip of craft motifs along the bottom edge.
- *App icon:* the bridge-hands symbol alone, simplified, on a terracotta rounded square; it still reads at 48 px.

**(c) 40-word explanation (4):**
> "Kala Setu's logo is a bridge made of two hands: an artisan's patterned hand and a buyer's hand meeting at the keystone. Beneath flow loom-like river lines. Terracotta and indigo signal earth, craft and trust, uniting village skill with city demand."

(40 words.)

**Rubric:** concept relevance 3, simplicity and scalability 2, craft/culture reference 2; applications 4 (2 each); explanation 4 (clarity 2, word limit 1, link to visuals 1).

### Q26. Heatwave design (20)

**(a) Journey map (6), user: delivery riders**

```
Time:   8am      11am         1pm            3pm             6pm
Step:  Login → Pickups → Peak-heat drops → Waiting at → Evening rush
                                         restaurants
Mood:   🙂       😐            😫               😣              😐
Pain:        (1) no shade   (2) dehydration  (3) no place to  (4) phone
             at pickup      & no water       rest/toilet      overheats,
             points         refill           while waiting    battery dies
```

Rubric: steps 2, at least 4 pain points 2, emotional curve 1, clarity 1.

**(b) Solution system (10), model: "Thanda Stop" + "CoolKit"**
- **Product, CoolKit:**
  - a helmet with a ventilated inner liner and a detachable neck-shade flap
  - an evaporative cooling vest (water-soaked inserts)
  - an insulated 1.5 L bottle mounted on the delivery box
  - a phone mount with a sun visor and air gap
- **Service, Thanda Stops:**
  - shaded kiosks at major food clusters, co-funded by delivery platforms and the municipality
  - free water refills, toilets, charging points and seating
  - an app feature that shows the nearest stop and allows "heat breaks" without ranking penalties between 1 and 4 pm
- **Policy hook:** rider ratings are not penalised for heat breaks during red-alert days.

Rubric: product feasibility 3, service design 3, links to the pain points 2, drawing and labels 2.

**(c) "After" scene (4):** a shaded kiosk with a green roof; riders refilling bottles; one charging a phone; a heat-index board showing "44 °C – Take a break"; riders wearing the CoolKit.
Rubric: shows use 2, clarity 1, context 1.
