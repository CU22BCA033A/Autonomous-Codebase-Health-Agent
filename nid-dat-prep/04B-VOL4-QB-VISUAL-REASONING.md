## A. Mirror & Water Images (1–20)

**VR-1.** How many of these capital letters look identical in a vertical mirror: A, B, C, M, T, Y, R, W?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 3
**Ans: (b)**. A, M, T, Y, W are symmetric about a vertical axis; B, C, R are not. *Concept:* vertical symmetry · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* the basic mirror skill · *Mistake:* counting B (which is symmetric about a horizontal axis, not a vertical one).

**VR-2.** Which word reads exactly the same in a vertical mirror?
(a) TOOT (b) BOOB (c) DEED (d) NOON
**Ans: (a)**. Two conditions are needed: the letters must be vertically symmetric, and the word must read the same backwards. TOOT meets both. B, D, E and N are not vertically symmetric. *Concept:* mirror words · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* combines two rules · *Mistake:* checking only the palindrome condition.

**VR-3.** Which word looks unchanged in a water image (top–bottom flip), letter positions unchanged?
(a) CHOICE (b) MOTHER (c) WATER (d) PAPER
**Ans: (a)**. C, H, O, I and E are all symmetric about a horizontal axis. In a water image the order of letters along the line is NOT reversed. *Concept:* water image · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* the classic item · *Mistake:* reversing the letter order.

**VR-4.** A clock shows 4:10. What time does its image in a vertical mirror show?
(a) 7:50 (b) 8:50 (c) 7:10 (d) 8:10
**Ans: (a)**. 11:60 − 4:10 = 7:50. *Concept:* mirror clock · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* spatial transformation · *Mistake:* answering 8:50 by subtracting the hours and minutes separately from 12:60.

**VR-5.** In a vertical mirror a clock appears to show 2:45. What is the actual time?
(a) 9:15 (b) 10:15 (c) 9:45 (d) 3:15
**Ans: (a)**. The relation is symmetric: 11:60 − 2:45 = 9:15. *Concept:* mirror clock (inverse) · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* reverse reasoning · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-6.** Which digit(s) look unchanged in a vertical mirror (simple digital-style digits)?
(a) 0, 1, 8 (b) 2, 5 (c) 3, 8 (d) 6, 9
**Ans: (a)**. In seven-segment form, 0, 8 and the centred "1" are vertically symmetric. 3 is symmetric only about a horizontal axis. *Concept:* digit symmetry · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* attention to form · *Mistake:* choosing 3.

**VR-7.** Which digits look unchanged in a water image (seven-segment style)?
(a) 0, 1, 3, 8 (b) 2, 5, 6 (c) 4, 7 (d) 6, 9
**Ans: (a)**. These are symmetric about a horizontal axis. 1 is a vertical stroke, so it stays the same when flipped top to bottom. *Concept:* horizontal symmetry · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* precision · *Mistake:* choosing 6/9 (that pair swaps under a 180° rotation, not under a water image).

**VR-8.** The word "NID" is held in front of a vertical mirror. In the mirror, the first letter you read from the left is:
(a) N (b) mirrored D (c) I (d) mirrored N
**Ans: (b)**. The order reverses, so D (now flipped) appears on the left. *Concept:* order reversal · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* the standard trap · *Mistake:* keeping the original order.

**VR-9.** An arrow points to the upper right (north-east). In a vertical mirror it points to:
(a) the upper right (b) the upper left (c) the lower right (d) the lower left
**Ans: (b)**. Left–right flips; up stays up. *Concept:* mirror of direction · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* transformation logic · *Mistake:* answering (d), which is a 180° rotation.

**VR-10.** The same north-east arrow in a water image points to:
(a) the upper left (b) the lower right (c) the lower left (d) unchanged
**Ans: (b)**. Up flips to down; right stays right. *Concept:* water image · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* paired with VR-9 · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-11.** A figure is reflected in a vertical mirror and then reflected again in a horizontal mirror. The net result equals:
(a) the original (b) a 90° rotation (c) a 180° rotation (d) a vertical-mirror image
**Ans: (c)**. Two perpendicular reflections compose to a 180° rotation. *Concept:* composition of transformations · *Diff:* H · *Why NID:* deeper spatial reasoning · *Mistake:* answering (a); two reflections across the *same* axis would give the original.

**VR-12.** How many letters of the word "EXHIBIT" look the same in a water image?
(a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 7 (d) 4
**Ans: (b)**. E, X, H, I, B, I are symmetric about a horizontal axis; T is not (its bar would move to the bottom). *Concept:* horizontal symmetry · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* counting with care · *Mistake:* counting T.

**VR-13.** Ambulances often have the word written as "ƎƆИA⅃UᗺMA" on the bonnet. Why?
(a) For style (b) So drivers ahead read it correctly in their rear-view mirrors (c) It's a printing error (d) It's another language
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* applied mirror design · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* links reasoning and real-world design · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-14.** In a vertical mirror, a person raises their right hand. The mirror image appears to raise:
(a) its right hand (b) its left hand (c) both hands (d) no hand
**Ans: (b)**. From the mirror figure's own point of view, it is raising its left hand. *Concept:* handedness reversal · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* spatial empathy · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-15.** A clock shows 12:20. Its vertical-mirror image shows:
(a) 11:40 (b) 12:40 (c) 11:20 (d) 10:40
**Ans: (a)**. For times between 11:00 and 12:59, use 23:60 − 12:20 = 11:40. Check: the minute hand at "4" (20 min) mirrors to "8" (40 min), and the hour hand just past 12 mirrors to just before 12, which gives 11:40 ✓. *Concept:* mirror clock edge case · *Diff:* H · *Why NID:* precision · *Mistake:* computing 11:60 − 12:20 as a negative number.

**VR-16.** Which letter looks identical in both a vertical mirror and a water image?
(a) A (b) B (c) X (d) E
**Ans: (c)**. X is symmetric about both axes. *Concept:* double symmetry · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* basic · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-17.** A car's number plate reads "MH 01". In a vertical mirror, which characters look the same as themselves (ignoring order)?
(a) M, H, 0, 1 (b) M, H only (c) H, 0 only (d) none
**Ans: (a)**. M, H, 0 and 1 are all vertically symmetric. The string order still reverses. *Concept:* character symmetry · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* observation · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-18.** Which pair of letters are mirror images of each other (vertical mirror) in standard lowercase?
(a) b and d (b) b and p (c) m and w (d) n and u
**Ans: (a)**. b and p are water images (top–bottom); n and u are 180° rotations; m and w are related by a 180° rotation or water image. *Concept:* letter transformations · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* fine discrimination · *Mistake:* choosing b/p.

**VR-19.** Which lowercase pair are water images of each other?
(a) b and d (b) b and p (c) p and q (d) d and q
**Ans: (b)**. b flipped top to bottom becomes p; d flipped becomes q. *Concept:* water image · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* pairs with VR-18 · *Mistake:* choosing p/q (those are vertical mirrors).

**VR-20.** A triangle points to the right (▶). Which transformation makes it point left (◀)? Choose ALL that work: (i) vertical mirror (ii) water image (iii) 180° rotation (iv) 90° rotation
(a) i only (b) i and iii (c) ii and iv (d) iii only
**Ans: (b)**. Both a left–right flip and a half turn reverse the direction of a symmetric arrow-triangle. *Concept:* equivalent transformations for symmetric shapes · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* flexible thinking · *Mistake:* stopping at one answer.

## B. Rotation & Symmetry (21–40)

**VR-21.** An arrow points North. It is rotated 90° clockwise three times, then 45° anticlockwise. Where does it point?
(a) North-West (b) West (c) South-West (d) North
**Ans: (c)**. 270° clockwise puts it at West. Turning 45° anticlockwise from West gives South-West. *Concept:* rotation arithmetic · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* mental rotation · *Mistake:* turning 45° in the wrong direction.

**VR-22.** Which of these words reads the same when the page is rotated 180°? (Use simple sans-serif capitals.)
(a) SWIMS (b) MOM (c) DAD (d) WOW
**Ans: (a)**. A 180° rotation reverses the letter order AND turns each letter upside down. S→S, W→M, I→I, M→W, so S-W-I-M-S becomes S-W-I-M-S ✓. MOM becomes WOW ✗, WOW becomes MOM ✗, and D upside-down is not D ✗. *Concept:* 180° ambigrams · *Diff:* H · *Why NID:* careful verification · *Mistake:* only checking whether the word is a palindrome.

**VR-23.** The number of lines of symmetry of a regular hexagon is:
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 6 (d) 12
**Ans: (c)**. Three lines pass through opposite vertices and three through midpoints of opposite sides. *Concept:* symmetry · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* geometry sense · *Mistake:* answering 3.

**VR-24.** How many lines of symmetry does a rectangle (not a square) have?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 0
**Ans: (b)**. The diagonals are NOT lines of symmetry for a non-square rectangle. *Concept:* symmetry · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* the classic trap · *Mistake:* answering 4.

**VR-25.** A parallelogram (not a rectangle or rhombus) has how many lines of symmetry and what order of rotational symmetry?
(a) 2 lines, order 2 (b) 0 lines, order 2 (c) 0 lines, order 1 (d) 1 line, order 2
**Ans: (b)**. It has no line symmetry, but a half turn maps it onto itself. *Concept:* line vs rotational symmetry · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* distinguishing the two kinds · *Mistake:* answering (a).

**VR-26.** Which letter has rotational symmetry of order 2 but NO line of symmetry?
(a) H (b) S (c) A (d) E
**Ans: (b)**. S looks the same after a half turn and has no mirror axis. H has both kinds of symmetry. *Concept:* symmetry types · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* discrimination · *Mistake:* choosing H.

**VR-27.** A five-pointed regular star has how many lines of symmetry?
(a) 5 (b) 10 (c) 2 (d) 1
**Ans: (a)**. *Concept:* symmetry · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* the star motif in logos · *Mistake:* answering 10.

**VR-28.** The Ashoka Chakra (24 equally spaced spokes) has rotational symmetry of order:
(a) 12 (b) 24 (c) 6 (d) 48
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* rotational symmetry · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* national symbol + geometry · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-29.** A square tile shows a single arrow pointing up. The tile is rotated 90° anticlockwise. Where does the arrow point?
(a) Right (b) Left (c) Down (d) Up
**Ans: (b)**. Turning anticlockwise, up goes to left. *Concept:* rotation direction · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* basics · *Mistake:* rotating clockwise by habit.

**VR-30.** The capital letter "F" is rotated 90° clockwise. What does it look like?
(a) A horizontal bar along the top, with two short strokes hanging down: one from the RIGHT end and one from the middle
(b) A horizontal bar along the top, with two short strokes hanging down: one from the LEFT end and one from the middle
(c) A horizontal bar along the bottom, with two short strokes pointing up from the right end and the middle
(d) It looks like the letter E
**Ans: (a)**. In F the stem is on the left and the arms point right from its top and middle. Turning 90° clockwise sends the top of the stem to the right, so the stem lies horizontal and the arms now point downward. The top arm hangs from the right end; the middle arm hangs from the middle. (b) is the mirror image of the correct answer, and (c) is the result of an anticlockwise turn followed by a flip. *Concept:* mental rotation · *Diff:* H · *Why NID:* precision · *Mistake:* choosing (b). A rotation can never produce a mirror image. (Check: write F on a sticky note and turn it.)

**VR-31.** A wheel with 8 equally spaced spokes is rotated by 135°. Does it look identical to the start?
(a) Yes (b) No
**Ans: (a)**. Order-8 symmetry means rotations by multiples of 45° look identical, and 135° = 3 × 45°. *Concept:* rotational symmetry · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* reasoning with angles · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-32.** Which shape tiles a floor alone with no gaps?
(a) Regular pentagon (b) Regular octagon (c) Regular hexagon (d) Circle
**Ans: (c)**. Its interior angle of 120° divides 360° exactly. *Concept:* tessellation · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* pattern design · *Mistake:* choosing the octagon (octagons need extra squares to fill the gaps).

**VR-33.** A clock's minute hand moves from 12 to 9 going clockwise. Through how many degrees has it turned?
(a) 90° (b) 180° (c) 270° (d) 360°
**Ans: (c)**. *Concept:* angle measurement · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* rotation · *Mistake:* answering 90° (that is the anticlockwise route).

**VR-34.** A design is made by rotating a single petal around a centre 6 times at equal intervals. The angle between petals is:
(a) 30° (b) 45° (c) 60° (d) 72°
**Ans: (c)**. 360° ÷ 6 = 60°. *Concept:* radial design · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* rangoli / mandala construction · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-35.** Which letter has exactly one horizontal line of symmetry and no vertical one?
(a) A (b) K (c) H (d) T
**Ans: (b)**. A and T have a vertical axis; H has both. *Concept:* symmetry · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* discrimination · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-36.** A semicircle has how many lines of symmetry?
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) infinite
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* symmetry · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* basics · *Mistake:* answering "infinite" by confusing it with a full circle.

**VR-37.** Which of these figures has rotational symmetry of order 3?
(a) Square (b) Equilateral triangle (c) Rectangle (d) Isosceles triangle
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* rotational symmetry · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* basics · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-38.** A 3×3 grid has only its top-left cell shaded. The grid is rotated 90° clockwise, then mirrored left–right. Which cell is shaded now?
(a) top-left (b) top-right (c) bottom-left (d) bottom-right
**Ans: (a)**. A 90° clockwise rotation moves top-left to top-right. The left–right mirror moves top-right back to top-left. *Concept:* composed transformations · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* multi-step tracking · *Mistake:* doing the steps in the wrong order.

**VR-39.** In the same 3×3 grid, the shaded cell is top-middle. After a 180° rotation it is:
(a) top-middle (b) bottom-middle (c) middle-left (d) centre
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* rotation · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* basics · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-40.** A paper strip has a half-twist and its ends are joined (a Möbius strip). How many sides and edges does it have?
(a) 2 sides, 2 edges (b) 1 side, 1 edge (c) 1 side, 2 edges (d) 2 sides, 1 edge
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* topology · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* curiosity / paper models (the recycling symbol is based on it) · *Mistake:* answering (a).

## C. Paper Folding & Cutting (41–60)

**VR-41.** A square sheet, 8 cm × 8 cm, is folded in half with the left half going onto the right half (fold line x = 4). A hole is punched at (6, 6). Unfolded, where are the holes?
(a) (6,6) only (b) (2,6) and (6,6) (c) (6,2) and (6,6) (d) (2,2) and (6,6)
**Ans: (b)**. Reflecting across x = 4 gives 4 − (6 − 4) = 2, with y unchanged. *Concept:* reflection across a fold · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* the basic fold · *Mistake:* reflecting y as well.

**VR-42.** The same 8×8 sheet is folded left onto right (x = 4), then the top onto the bottom (y = 4). One hole is punched at (5, 3). How many holes appear, and where?
(a) 2 (b) 4: (5,3), (3,3), (5,5), (3,5) (c) 4: (5,3), (3,3), (5,1), (3,1) (d) 8
**Ans: (b)**. Unfold in reverse order. First undo the top-onto-bottom fold: (5,3) reflects across y = 4 to (5,5). Then undo the left-onto-right fold: (5,3)→(3,3) and (5,5)→(3,5). *Concept:* reverse unfolding · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* the core skill · *Mistake:* reflecting across the wrong axis.

**VR-43.** A sheet is folded in half 4 times, and a hole is punched through all layers once (not on a fold). How many holes appear when unfolded?
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 16 (d) 32
**Ans: (c)**. 2⁴ = 16. *Concept:* layer doubling · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* the quick rule · *Mistake:* answering 8.

**VR-44.** A square sheet is folded along its diagonal from bottom-left to top-right (the top-left triangle goes onto the bottom-right). A hole is punched at (6, 2) on an 8×8 sheet. Where is the second hole after unfolding?
(a) (2,6) (b) (6,6) (c) (2,2) (d) (6,2) only
**Ans: (a)**. Reflection across the line y = x swaps the coordinates: (6,2) → (2,6). *Concept:* diagonal fold · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* the harder fold type · *Mistake:* treating it as a vertical fold.

**VR-45.** A rectangle is folded in half once, and a semicircle is cut out with its flat side on the fold line. Unfolded, the hole is:
(a) a semicircle (b) a full circle (c) two semicircles at opposite edges (d) an ellipse
**Ans: (b)**. The cut mirrors across the fold to complete a circle. *Concept:* cut on a fold · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* paper cutting · *Mistake:* answering (c).

**VR-46.** A paper is folded in half twice (making 4 layers, folds meeting at one corner). A quarter-circle is cut from the corner where both folds meet. Unfolded, the result is:
(a) a quarter-circle notch (b) a full circular hole in the centre (c) four circles (d) a semicircle on an edge
**Ans: (b)**. The corner where the folds meet is the centre of the original sheet, so four quarter-circles join into a full circle. *Concept:* the snowflake principle · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* the classic item · *Mistake:* answering (c).

**VR-47.** A long strip of paper is folded in half 3 times, each fold parallel to the previous one (making 8 layers). It is then cut with ONE straight cut across all layers, parallel to the folds but not on a fold. How many pieces result?
(a) 2 (b) 5 (c) 8 (d) 9
**Ans: (d)**. Each cut through the folded packet separates the strip at every layer, giving 8 cut lines on the unfolded strip and therefore 8 + 1 = 9 pieces. Test with small cases: 1 fold (2 layers) gives 3 pieces, and 2 folds (4 layers) give 5. The pattern is 2ⁿ + 1. *Concept:* fold-and-cut reasoning · *Diff:* H · *Why NID:* deeper reasoning · *Mistake:* answering 8 (the layers, not the pieces).

**VR-48.** A paper is folded like a fan (accordion) into 4 equal vertical strips. A small triangle is cut out of the CENTRE of the folded stack (not touching any edge or fold). How many triangular holes appear when unfolded?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 3
**Ans: (c)**. The accordion makes 4 layers, so the cut passes through 4 layers and gives 4 holes. Neighbouring holes are mirror images of each other across the folds. *Concept:* accordion fold · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* garland and paper-chain designs · *Mistake:* answering 1.

**VR-49.** Paper doll chains (people holding hands) are made by accordion-folding and cutting a figure whose hands touch the folded edges. Why must the hands touch the folds?
(a) For decoration (b) Uncut fold segments keep the figures connected (c) To save paper (d) There is no reason
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* the fold as a hinge or connection · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* craft logic · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-50.** An 8×8 sheet is folded so that the top edge meets the bottom edge (fold at y = 4), then the right edge meets the left edge (fold at x = 4). A hole is punched at (1, 1) in the folded packet, which spans x 0–4 and y 0–4. Unfolded holes:
(a) (1,1), (7,1), (1,7), (7,7) (b) (1,1), (3,1), (1,3), (3,3) (c) (1,1) only (d) (1,1), (1,7)
**Ans: (a)**. Undo the right-onto-left fold: (1,1)→(7,1). Undo the top-onto-bottom fold: each point reflects across y = 4, so (1,1)→(1,7) and (7,1)→(7,7). *Concept:* reverse unfolding · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* practice · *Mistake:* reflecting across x = 2.

**VR-51.** Which fold produces a mirror-symmetric shape with a vertical axis when cutting a heart?
(a) Fold vertically and cut half a heart along the fold (b) Fold horizontally (c) Fold diagonally (d) No fold
**Ans: (a)**. *Concept:* symmetric cutting · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* craft · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-52.** A sheet is folded in half. A square punch is placed so that it straddles the folded edge (half of the punch over the paper, half hanging off the fold). Unfolded, you see:
(a) two separate square holes (b) one rectangular hole twice as wide as the part of the punch that bit into the paper, centred on the fold line (c) a notch on the outer edge (d) no hole
**Ans: (b)**. The punch cuts a notch into the folded edge. Unfolding mirrors the notch across the fold, joining the two halves into one hole that straddles the fold line. *Concept:* punch on a fold · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* the typical trap · *Mistake:* answering (a).

**VR-53.** How many layers result from folding a sheet in half, then in thirds (accordion, 3 panels)?
(a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) 9
**Ans: (b)**. 2 × 3 = 6. *Concept:* layer multiplication · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* reasoning · *Mistake:* adding instead of multiplying.

**VR-54.** A square paper is folded in half twice to form a smaller square (4 layers). One small circle is punched at the centre of this small square (not on any fold). How many holes appear after unfolding, and how are they arranged?
(a) 4, one at the centre of each quadrant (b) 4, clustered at the centre (c) 1 at the centre (d) 2
**Ans: (a)**. The centre of the folded square maps to the centre of each quarter of the original sheet. *Concept:* mapping folded positions · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* practice · *Mistake:* answering (b).

**VR-55.** Origami is the Japanese art of paper folding. Kirigami differs because it:
(a) uses glue (b) involves cutting as well as folding (c) uses cloth (d) uses no folds
**Ans: (b)**. *Concept:* paper craft vocabulary · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* the studio test · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-56.** A strip of paper is folded in half 5 times along its length, then unfolded. How many crease lines are there?
(a) 5 (b) 16 (c) 31 (d) 32
**Ans: (c)**. Folding 5 times creates 2⁵ = 32 segments, separated by 31 creases. *Concept:* creases = segments − 1 · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* reasoning · *Mistake:* answering 32.

**VR-57.** An 8×8 square is folded diagonally from the top-left corner to the bottom-right corner (the bottom-left triangle folds onto the top-right triangle). A hole is punched at (6, 6). The second hole after unfolding is at:
(a) (2,2) (b) (6,6) only (c) (2,6) (d) (6,2)
**Ans: (a)**. The fold line runs from (0,8) to (8,0), i.e. x + y = 8. Reflecting (6,6) across it gives (8 − 6, 8 − 6) = (2,2). *Concept:* reflection across the anti-diagonal · *Diff:* H · *Why NID:* advanced folding · *Mistake:* swapping coordinates as if the fold were y = x.

**VR-58.** A circular paper (a coffee filter) is folded in half, then in half again, then once more, making 8 layers in a narrow wedge. One small hole is punched in the interior of the wedge, not touching any edge or fold. How many holes appear after unfolding?
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 16 (d) 2
**Ans: (b)**. 8 layers give 8 holes, arranged in a ring with radial symmetry. *Concept:* radial cutting (snowflakes) · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* radial design · *Mistake:* answering 4.

**VR-59.** To make a 6-pointed paper snowflake, the circle or square should be folded so that the wedge angle is:
(a) 90° (b) 60° (c) 30° (d) 45°
**Ans: (c)**. Six-fold symmetry with mirror lines needs 12 identical mirrored wedges, and 360° ÷ 12 = 30°. (A 60° wedge gives 6 identical parts, but the mirror symmetry comes from folding the wedge in half.) *Concept:* radial symmetry construction · *Diff:* H · *Why NID:* deeper craft geometry · *Mistake:* answering 60°.

**VR-60.** A sheet folded in half twice, at right angles, has a hole punched through all layers. One of the unfolded holes is at the top-left quadrant's centre. Where are the others?
(a) The centres of the other three quadrants (b) Along the top edge only (c) The centre of the sheet only (d) Random
**Ans: (a)**. *Concept:* symmetry about both fold lines · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* reinforcement · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-63.** In the net below, which face is opposite face C?
```
   [A]
[B][C][D][E]
   [F]
```

## H. Spatial Relations, Gears, Maps & Mixed (136–150)

**VR-136.** Gear A (turning clockwise) meshes with Gear B, which meshes with Gear C, in a line. Which way does Gear C turn?
(a) Clockwise (b) Anticlockwise (c) It doesn't move (d) It alternates
**Ans: (a)**. Meshing gears alternate direction: A clockwise, B anticlockwise, C clockwise. *Concept:* mechanical reasoning · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* product mechanisms · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-137.** Three gears are arranged in a closed triangle, each meshing with the other two. What happens when one is turned?

**Ans: (b)**. An odd closed loop forces a direction conflict. *Concept:* mechanical logic · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* reasoning · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-138.** Gear A has 20 teeth and drives Gear B with 60 teeth. If A turns 6 times, how many times does B turn?
(a) 18 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 6
**Ans: (b)**. Teeth passed = 20 × 6 = 120, and 120 ÷ 60 = 2. *Concept:* gear ratio · *Diff:* M · *Why NID:* mechanism thinking · *Mistake:* answering 18.

**VR-139.** Two pulleys are connected by an open (uncrossed) belt. If the first turns clockwise, the second turns:

**Ans: (a)**. An open belt keeps the same direction; a crossed belt reverses it. *Concept:* pulleys · *Diff:* E · *Why NID:* mechanisms · *Mistake:* none.

**VR-140.** You face North, turn right, turn right again, then turn left. Which direction do you face?

**VR-141.** On a map, North is up. A temple is 3 km east and 4 km north of a school. What is the straight-line distance?

**VR-142.** A person walks 5 m forward, turns 90° left and walks 5 m, turns 90° left and walks 5 m, then turns 90° left and walks 5 m. Where are they?

**VR-143.** In a row of 5 objects, a cup is 2nd from the left and a vase is 2nd from the right. How many objects lie between them?

**VR-144.** A ladder leans against a wall with its top at 4 m and its foot 3 m from the wall. How long is the ladder?

**VR-145.** A lever: a 10 kg load sits 1 m from the fulcrum. What force is needed at 2 m on the other side to balance it?

**VR-146.** A bicycle's front gear (chainring) has 48 teeth and the rear sprocket has 16. For each pedal turn, the rear wheel turns:

**VR-147.** Which knot or join holds best when two sticks must be tied at right angles for a bamboo frame?

**VR-148.** Which structure is strongest for a paper bridge using the same amount of paper?

**VR-149.** Triangles are used in bridge trusses and roof frames because:
