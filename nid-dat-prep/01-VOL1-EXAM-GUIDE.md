## 2. Stage-by-stage walkthrough

### 2.1 Application
- Apply online at admissions.nid.edu. You need a photo, a signature, the Class 10 certificate (for date of birth), a category certificate if applicable, and a PwD certificate if applicable.
- Choose your programme(s) and campus preferences carefully. **Read the 2027-28 Handbook's rules on whether you can apply to both the B.Des and the Integrated Pathway, and how preferences are filled.** [Verify on portal]
- Choose a test city you can reach easily. Prelims is offline.

### 2.2 DAT Prelims (20 Dec 2026)
**Materials usually allowed [REPORTED from past instructions; verify on admit card]:** pencils (HB, 2B, 4B, 6B), eraser, sharpener, scale, colour pencils, crayons or oil pastels, sketch pens, and a black pen. Water colours or poster colours have been permitted in some years. **Always follow the admit card.** Blank papers are generally not allowed; you work in the answer booklet.

**Two formats to be ready for:**

| Format A: Handbook scheme | Format B: 2026 actual |
|---|---|
| Part I: 22 objective (30 marks) | No objective part |
| Part II: 4 subjective (70 marks) | 6 subjective questions, ~14 sub-tasks (100 marks) |
| Time split: ~25 min Part I, ~150 min Part II | ~12 min per sub-task, ~10 min buffer |

### 2.3 DAT Mains: Studio Sensitivity Test
**What candidates have reported across years [REPORTED]:**
- Make a 3D model from clay, paper, card, thread, wire, foam or found objects on a theme (e.g. "movement", "a creature from your imagination", "a toy for a blind child").
- Sketch an object placed in front of you; observe and draw it from different views.
- Respond to an audio clip or video by drawing or writing.
- Material exploration: "Make as many textures as you can with this paper."
- Group or individual tasks that show the process as well as the result.

**What it tests:** hand skill, material sensitivity, 3D thinking, originality, and the ability to explain your idea.

### 2.4 DAT Mains: In-Person Sensitivity Test
- An interaction with a faculty panel. Common themes [REPORTED]:
  - why design
  - observations from your hometown
  - a product you admire and why
  - crafts of your state
  - current design news
  - your hobbies
  - your Studio Test model
- Preparation is covered in Volume 3 §12 and Volume 11.

## 4. Marking and evaluation: what examiners reward

NID has not published a rubric. The criteria below are **[INFERRED]** from NID's stated aims (design aptitude, creativity, visualisation, observation, problem-solving) and from patterns across years.

| Criterion | What it looks like on paper | Common way to lose marks |
|---|---|---|
| **Understanding the question** | You answer exactly what is asked: the right number of frames, signboards, the viewpoint | Drawing 3 signboards when 5 are asked; ignoring "write 200 words" |
| **Idea quality / originality** | A fresh but sensible interpretation | The first cliché idea (e.g. "save trees" with a crying tree) |
| **Observation** | Believable details from real life: the bucket under the tap, the scooter's mirror, slippers outside the door | Generic, empty scenes |
| **Communication** | A viewer understands the drawing in about 5 seconds; labels and arrows where they help | Cluttered or ambiguous drawings |
| **Composition** | Clear focal point, use of the whole space, varied scale | A tiny drawing in the centre of a huge box |
| **Proportion & perspective** | People sized correctly against doors and vehicles; a consistent horizon | Floating people, tilted floors |
| **Empathy / user focus** | The solution fits the user (elderly people, children, vendors) | "Cool" ideas that ignore the user |
| **Craft (secondary)** | Clean lines, purposeful shading and colour | Over-rendering one corner and leaving the rest unfinished |
| **Completion** | Every task attempted | Leaving the last question blank |

**Photorealism is NOT required.** A clear, confident, well-observed sketch with a good idea scores higher than a beautiful drawing of a weak idea. [INFERRED from consistent NID messaging and the nature of the questions]

## 5. The complete NID DAT syllabus [INFERRED: NID publishes none]

Priority key: **VH** = very high, **H** = high, **M** = medium, **L** = low (for the Dec 2026 Prelims).

### A. Design Aptitude (VH)
- **Observation**: noticing details, proportion and context. VH
- **Visual perception**: figure–ground, gestalt (proximity, similarity, closure, continuity), illusions. H
- **Visual reasoning**: reading meaning from images. H
- **Design thinking**: empathise → define → ideate → prototype → test. VH
- **Problem solving**: finding the real problem, constraints, trade-offs. VH
- **Creative thinking**: fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration. VH
- **Lateral thinking**: unusual viewpoints, reversal, random stimulus. VH
- **Divergent vs convergent thinking**: many ideas, then select one. H
- **Functional thinking**: form follows function; affordances. H
- **User-centred thinking**: personas, accessibility, inclusivity. VH
- **Innovation**: incremental vs radical; frugal design (jugaad, used critically). M
- **Aesthetics**: harmony, proportion, colour, style. H
- **Usability**: learnability, efficiency, error prevention, feedback. H
- **Ergonomics**: anthropometry, reach, posture, grip. M
- **Sustainability**: reduce, reuse, recycle, repair; life-cycle; materials. H

### B. Visual Reasoning (H if Part I appears; M otherwise)
- **Patterns, series, figure completion, odd one out, visual analogy**: H
- **Mirror and water images; rotation; reflection**: H
- **Paper folding, cutting and unfolding**: H
- **2D↔3D: cubes, nets, dice, isometric↔orthographic views**: H
- **Spatial relations; hidden and embedded figures; counting figures**: M
- **Perspective, shadows, silhouettes**: H (these also feed drawing)

### C. Quantitative Aptitude (L–M)
Basic arithmetic, percentages, ratio, averages, fractions, units, time and distance, area, perimeter, volume, number series, logical puzzles.
**How much maths does NID need?** Very little. Class 8–10 level is enough. In MCQ years there were usually only 1–4 maths-flavoured items, mostly geometry, area or proportion embedded in a visual context (e.g. "how many tiles cover this floor?"). **Do not** prepare CAT/JEE-level maths. Spend at most 5% of your time here.

### D. Logical Reasoning (M)
Sequences, arrangements, analogies, classification, coding-decoding, syllogisms, statement–assumption, cause–effect, visual logic, puzzles.

### E. Verbal Ability (L–M)
Vocabulary, comprehension, idioms, interpreting statements, and **descriptive writing** (H for the subjective part).

### F. General Awareness, design-focused (M)
Indian crafts and textiles, folk and tribal art, architecture, designers, artists, logos, iconic products, design movements, museums, institutions, awards, UNESCO / GI, sustainability, AI and design.

### G. Current Affairs (L for Prelims, M for the interview)
The last 12–18 months of design, architecture, art, tech, AI, environment and culture.

### H. Drawing & Visualisation (VH): the core of the paper
Line, shape, form, proportion, perspective (1-, 2- and 3-point), human figures and gestures, memory drawing, scenes, storyboards, product sketching, light and shadow, texture, materials, colour application, lettering.

## 6. What previous NID papers teach us

### 6.1 Pattern evolution: older → recent

```
2016–2019  ──►  MCQ-heavy GAT (GK, maths, logic, English, design awareness)
               + 3–5 drawing questions (memory drawing, poster, symbol)
2020–2022  ──►  Fewer pure-GK MCQs; more visual reasoning & design awareness;
               drawing still ~60–70 marks; "what if" + empathy prompts rise
2023–2025  ──►  22–27 objective items (30–40 marks), heavily visual (logos,
               spatial, observation); 4 big subjective tasks (60–70 marks)
               Ex (2025): poster from a crow's viewpoint; neem-branch neighbour dispute
2026       ──►  NO objective part. 6 long subjective questions (~14 tasks):
               empathy sequences (elderly fall), travel kit for grandmother,
               "product you love" + 200 words, Indian drink illustration,
               5 signboards for narrow streets, scene/perspective, redesign
2027       ──►  [PREDICTION] Subjective-dominant. Objective part may or may
               not return. Everyday India + empathy + communication.
```
All year-specific details are [REPORTED] (memory-based). The arrows describe a [TREND-BASED] reading.

### 6.2 Recurring question types, with an analysis of each

**1. Scene drawing with people (markets, stations, homes, festivals)**
- *Frequency:* almost every year. [TREND-BASED]
- *How it changed:* "draw a railway platform" has become "draw a three-storey scene", "a narrow street with signboards" or "a view from a specific viewpoint".
- *Skill tested:* perspective, proportion, observation, composition, storytelling.
- *Common misunderstanding:* students think it is about drawing skill alone. It is about **believable life**: activity, interaction, details.
- *How to prepare:* 1 scene a day from observation or memory; learn 1- and 2-point perspective; draw people in 30-second gestures.

**2. Sequential drawing / storyboard**
- *Frequency:* very frequent, and growing.
- *How it changed:* from neutral processes ("making tea") to empathy and emotion ("helping an elderly person who falls").
- *Skill:* narrative, continuity (same characters and setting across frames), camera angles, emotion.
- *Misunderstanding:* drawing unrelated frames, or changing the character's look between frames.
- *Prepare:* 3/4/6-frame stories twice a week. Vary the shots (wide, medium, close-up).

**3. Product redesign / problem-solving**
- *Frequency:* very frequent. Examples: the neem branch dispute (2025), a train-related problem (2025), redesigning a product (2026).
- *Skill:* problem identification, user empathy, feasible ideas, explaining with labelled drawings.
- *Misunderstanding:* jumping to a gadget. Examiners want **problem → insight → solution**.
- *Prepare:* the "Problem–User–Constraint–Idea–Sketch" drill (Vol 3 §8).

**4. Visual communication: posters, signage, symbols, logos, packaging**
- *Frequency:* very frequent. Examples: an orange-juice poster from a crow's viewpoint (2025); 5 signboards for narrow streets (2026).
- *Skill:* hierarchy, colour, typography, simplification, clarity at a distance.
- *Misunderstanding:* adding too much text or detail. Signage must work in 2 seconds.
- *Prepare:* learn pictogram rules (Vol 2A §6) and design 3 posters or symbols a week.

**5. Memory drawing + writing**
- *Frequency:* frequent. Example: "a product you love + 200 words" (2026).
- *Skill:* recall accuracy, descriptive language, reasoning about design qualities.
- *Prepare:* draw 5 everyday objects from memory each week, then check them against the real thing.

**6. Unusual viewpoints / imagination ("what if", "if you were an ant")**
- *Frequency:* frequent.
- *Skill:* lateral thinking, scale manipulation, worm's-eye and bird's-eye perspective.
- *Prepare:* the viewpoint drills in Vol 3 §5.

**7. Visual reasoning MCQs: folding, cubes, mirror images, counting, patterns**
- *Frequency:* every year with Part I (to 2025); absent in 2026.
- *Skill:* spatial visualisation.
- *Prepare:* Vol 4B. Practise until accuracy is above 85%.

**8. Logos / design GK MCQs**
- *Frequency:* frequent in Part I years (logo identification in 2025).
- *Prepare:* the logo table in Vol 2C and the flashcards in Vol 12.

**9. Colour application**
- *Frequency:* moderate; often embedded ("use only warm colours", "monochrome").
- *Prepare:* the colour theory section of Vol 2A, plus colouring practice with limited palettes.

**10. Creative writing / captions / slogans**
- *Frequency:* moderate and rising.
- *Prepare:* timed 150–200-word descriptions; taglines of 3–6 words.

## 8. Exam-day strategy & time management

### 8.1 Before the exam
- Sleep 7–8 hours. Pack the admit card, photo ID, and a pencil pouch with spares: 2 HB, 2B, 4B, 6B, eraser, kneaded eraser, sharpener, 30 cm scale, 24-shade colour pencils, oil pastels, sketch pens and 2 black fine-liners. Bring a watch if allowed.
- Reach the centre 45 minutes early.

### 8.2 The first 10 minutes (both formats)
1. Read the **whole paper** once. Circle the counts ("5 signboards", "4 frames", "200 words") and the constraints ("only 3 colours", "bird's-eye view").
2. Write a time budget next to each question (marks ÷ 100 × 165 minutes).
3. Note 2–3 quick idea keywords beside each subjective question. Your brain keeps working on them in the background.

### 8.3 Time budgets

**Format A (22 objective + 4 subjective, 180 min):**
| Block | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Read the paper | 5 |
| Part I, Q1–22 (30 marks) | 25 (skip any item over 90 sec, return later) |
| Q23 (15) | 28 |
| Q24 (20) | 38 |
| Q25 (15) | 28 |
| Q26 (20) | 38 |
| Buffer: finishing, labels, unanswered MCQs | 18 |

**Format B (6 questions, ~14 sub-tasks, 100 marks):**
- Minutes per sub-task ≈ 165 ÷ number of sub-tasks (≈ 12 min each for 14).
- Use the **70% rule**: once a drawing is 70% done, move on. Come back to polish only if time remains.
- **Never leave a task blank.** A 3-minute rough sketch with labels earns marks; a blank earns zero.

### 8.4 Guessing policy
No negative marking (officially), so **answer every MCQ**. Eliminate options first, then guess.

### 8.5 The five golden rules for subjective answers
1. **Answer the question asked**: count, viewpoint, medium, user.
2. **Idea first, rendering second.** Spend 2–3 minutes thinking before drawing.
3. **Fill the box with purpose**: a clear focal point and a sensible scale.
4. **Label smartly**: short labels and arrows for mechanisms and materials. Write a 1–2 line note explaining the idea if allowed.
5. **Finish everything**, then add colour or shading to your strongest piece.
