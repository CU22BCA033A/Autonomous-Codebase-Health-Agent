## 4. Exam stages & official pattern

### Stage 1: DAT Prelims (shortlisting only)

**What the official Handbook text says** [OFFICIAL, per handbook extracts for 2026-27 and quoted again for 2027-28]:

| Part | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Part I: objective | Q1–14 at 1 mark each (14) + Q15–22 at 2 marks each (16) | **30** |
| Part II: subjective | Q23 = 15, Q24 = 20, Q25 = 15, Q26 = 20 | **70** |
| **Total** | 26 questions, **3 hours** | **100**, **no negative marking** |

- **The Prelims score is used only to shortlist for Mains. It does NOT carry weightage into the final merit list.** [OFFICIAL: "Prelims score will not be carried forward as any weightage to the DAT Mains stage"]
- Last cycle shortlisted roughly **1.5 candidates per seat** for B.Des Mains. [REPORTED]

**What actually happened in Dec 2025 (DAT 2026)** [REPORTED by Careers360, Shiksha, GetMyUni and others]:
- **There was no objective / General Aptitude section.** The paper was fully subjective.
- It had **6 main questions with 2–3 sub-parts each, about 14 drawing/writing tasks** in 3 hours. Students found it lengthy rather than conceptually hard ("moderate to difficult").
- Memory-based questions include:
  - a sequential drawing of helping an elderly person who falls during a morning walk
  - sketching the items to carry when travelling with your grandmother
  - drawing a product you love and writing about it in 200 words
  - illustrating shikanji / thandai (an Indian drink)
  - designing 5 signboards for narrow streets
  - also reported: a three-storey scene, completing an everyday object, redesigning a product, a perspective drawing, a process drawing

**Bottom line for 2027 [INFERRED]:** the Handbook's printed scheme (22 objective + 4 subjective) and the real 2026 paper (all subjective) do **not** match. NID has the right to change the paper format. **Prepare for both.** The safe strategy is 75–80% of prep time on drawing, ideation and design thinking, with the objective skills (visual reasoning, design GK) kept warm. Those skills also feed the subjective answers: you cannot draw a good "Warli-inspired signboard" without knowing Warli.

### Stage 2: DAT Mains (decides final merit)

| Component | What it involves | Weight (last cycle) |
|---|---|---|
| **Studio Sensitivity Test** | Hands-on: sketching, doodling, material handling, 3D model-making with given materials (clay, paper, wire, found objects), sometimes audio-visual prompts. 2026: ~2.5 hours (9:30–12:00). | **60%** [REPORTED] |
| **In-Person Sensitivity Test** | Face-to-face interaction / interview-style assessment of awareness, communication, motivation and portfolio-type thinking | **40%** [REPORTED] |

- For 2027-28, one portal says the "exact split [is] to be announced". Treat 60:40 as the most recent known split, not a confirmed one. [REPORTED]
- **Final merit = 100% Mains.** [OFFICIAL in principle, from the "Prelims not carried forward" rule]

### Official syllabus
**NID does not publish a topic-wise syllabus.** [REPORTED by several portals; consistent with the Handbooks] The Handbook describes the test only in broad terms: design aptitude, creativity, visualisation, observation and problem-solving. Every "syllabus" in this package is therefore **[INFERRED]** from papers.

### Cutoffs
NID does not publish official subject-wise cutoffs in a form I could verify. Coaching estimates for 2026 (General ~50–55, OBC ~45, SC/ST ~40 out of 100) are **[UNVERIFIED]** and are not used as targets anywhere in this package.

## 5. Previous-paper trends (summary; full analysis in Volume 1 §6 and Volume 5)

| Year (exam held) | Structure | Character | Source status |
|---|---|---|---|
| DAT 2019–2022 | MCQ-heavy Part A (GK, visual reasoning, design awareness, some maths) + drawing Part B | GAT and CAT roughly balanced | [REPORTED] |
| DAT 2024 (Dec 2023) | 31 Qs: Section 1 = Q1–27 MCQ (40 marks); Section 2 = Q28–31 subjective (60 marks) | easy–moderate; visual reasoning and design awareness | [REPORTED] |
| DAT 2025 (Dec 2024) | 22 objective + 4 subjective = 100 | CAT gained weight; logo identification; a poster for an orange-juice corner from a **crow's point of view**; a **neem branch overhanging two neighbours** problem; a train-related problem question | [REPORTED] |
| **DAT 2026 (Dec 2025)** | **6 subjective Qs (~14 tasks); no MCQs** | lengthy; empathy, everyday Indian life, sequence, signage, product love, food illustration | [REPORTED] |
| **DAT 2027 (20 Dec 2026)** | **Unknown.** Handbook still prints the 22+4 scheme. | Prepare for a subjective-heavy paper | [PREDICTION] |

**Clear long-run trend [TREND-BASED]:** the paper moves away from recalling facts and towards **observation of everyday Indian life + empathy + problem-solving, communicated through quick, clear drawings and short writing.**

## 6. Most important topics (ranked)

1. **Scene drawing with people in perspective** (markets, streets, homes, stations, multi-storey views)
2. **Sequential / storyboard drawing** (3–6 frames showing an event or process, often with empathy: elderly people, children, accidents, help)
3. **Product redesign / problem-solving** (identify user problems, then sketch the solution with labels)
4. **Visual communication** (posters, signage, pictograms, symbols, logos, packaging) with colour and typography
5. **Memory and observation drawing** (objects, food, vehicles, an Indian context)
6. **Creative / lateral thinking** (unusual points of view such as a crow's eye or an ant's eye; "what if"; combining objects)
7. **Short descriptive or persuasive writing** (100–200 words about a product or experience)
8. **Visual reasoning** (folding, cubes, mirror images, patterns, counting): needed if Part I returns
9. **Design & cultural awareness** (Indian crafts, textiles, art forms, architecture, logos, designers)
10. **Current design affairs**: the low-probability MCQ topic, but useful for the interview

## 7. Most likely question categories [PREDICTION]

- **Tier A (very high):** a scene with people; a sequential story with an empathy angle; redesign or solve an everyday problem; a poster or signage set; drawing from memory plus a description
- **Tier B (high):** pictograms or symbols; packaging; unusual viewpoints; object completion or transformation; colour-scheme application; writing 150–200 words
- **Tier C (moderate):** an MCQ block (visual reasoning, logos, crafts, designers); maths-lite puzzles; current affairs

## 8. Major changes from older NID papers

1. **Objective GK/aptitude shrank, then vanished (in 2026).**
2. **Prelims lost its weightage** in the final merit; it is now only a gate.
3. **Questions became longer and multi-part.** Time management now decides results.
4. **Indian everyday context dominates:** grandparents, street food, narrow lanes, neighbours, trains.
5. **Writing appears alongside drawing** (justify, describe, label).
6. **A new 5.5-year Integrated Pathway** joins B.Des in 2027-28, using the same Prelims.
